# 2026-10-03

## New books in a series

The library now picks up a series' next book on its own. The API has no series endpoint. Work details
carry `seriesWorkIds`, the whole series in order, and library entries carry `seriesId`, so one details
call per series is enough. `/v1/catalog/search?series=<id>` answers `NotFound`.

A new book goes to the service's Saved shelf rather than into the store alone, because the next library
fetch replaces the store's shelves wholesale. Once there, it arrives the way any book added elsewhere
does, as a cover for a day.

What counts as new is whatever a series lists that the device hasn't seen in it before. Comparing
against the books on the shelf alone would have brought back every book the reader had removed, and
the whole rest of any series they'd only started.

## Alerts for new chapters and books

The app used to ask for the badge alone, and now asks for alerts as well. iOS asks a reader only once,
so a device that already answered the badge prompt won't show alerts until they're switched on under
Settings, Notifications, Bookhold.

`%lld new chapters` now has plural forms in both languages. The book row's accessibility label shares
the key, and it used to say "1 new chapters".

## The design system screen

The UI tab ran off both sides of the phone. Six covers stood in one row, wider than the screen, and
the scroll view took that width for everything in it. The covers now wrap.

The cover with no artwork had its fore-edge rounded far too much, and its foot's shade was stretched
up the board. `CoverPrint.blank` is a small picture with cap insets, so only its flat middle stretches,
but SwiftUI's `Image(uiImage:).resizable()` ignores them and scales the whole thing. `CoverImage` now
passes them to `resizable(capInsets:)`. Every row cover still waiting for its artwork had the same
fault; the shelf draws through `UIImageView`, which respects the insets.

The indeterminate `ProgressBar` bounced between the ends of its track, stalling at each. Its piece
now runs in at the leading end and out at the trailing one at a steady pace, on a `TimelineView`
clock and clipped to the track. With easing, it slowed most where it was half off the track and
lingered there as a sliver.
