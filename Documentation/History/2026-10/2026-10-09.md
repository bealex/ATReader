# 9 October 2026

## A faded picture on a dark page

Nothing shipped from this. It's an investigation, kept so the next attempt starts where this one
stopped.

A drawing that fades out to white melts into a light page and stands on a dark one as a pale block.
Inverting it isn't an answer, since that turns its lights into shadows. What's wanted is the white
around the picture replaced by the page's colour, with every tone inside left as it was.

The prototypes, the contact sheets and the scripts that made them are in the gitignored
`Fixtures/Reports/paper-in-dark-mode/`. They hold pictures out of books, so nothing there is
committed.

### What the pictures are

Three kinds turned up in the books tried, and they don't want the same treatment.

- **Ink on paper.** Black line work, often stored as black on transparency. The reader already reads
  darkness as ink and draws it in the page's two colours.
- **A shaded drawing that fades to paper.** Grey or colour work with a soft edge. The paper is a real
  background here, and it's the case this was about.
- **A picture cut at its edge.** A full scene, sometimes inside a thin white margin. There's no fade
  to follow and only the margin is paper.

### Networks don't answer it

Apple's subject model (`VNGenerateForegroundInstanceMaskRequest`) keeps what it takes for the subject
and drops the rest of the drawing: lettering, rain, grass, a room, a forest under a ship, a planet
behind one. It found nothing at all in several plates. About 20 ms a picture on the Mac. A picture
stored on transparency has to be flattened onto white first, or the model sees black on black.

`sbb_binarization` separates ink from paper. On line work it returns the picture it was given, since
the file already says where the ink is. On a shaded drawing it turns every grey into black or white.
The model is 148 MB, TF-Keras, Apache-2.0, and took about 9 seconds a picture from the command line,
most of that loading it.

Neither describes a soft fade. A subject model looks for objects and a binarizer for ink, and the
paper in a faded drawing is neither: it's wherever the paint thins out.

Models read about and not run: BiRefNet and `anime-segmentation` remove backgrounds the way Apple's
does, `MangaLineExtraction` pulls structural lines, and `docExtractor` finds the plate on a scanned
page, which a book's file already knows.

### The paper share

A faded picture is paint over white with a coverage that falls to nothing at the edge, so a pixel is
`paint × coverage + white × (1 − coverage)`. Call `1 − coverage` the paper share. Changing the paper
is then one subtraction, and the paint is never touched:

    new = old − share × (white − page)

Everything turns on estimating the share. A light pixel is either thin paint over paper or light
paint, and the picture alone can't say which, so each method is a rule for guessing.

| Method | On the faded drawings |
| --- | --- |
| Multiply by the page colour, so all lightness is paper | The whole picture goes nearly black |
| White joined to the edge through light pixels, a pixel at a time | The fade is right, and faces and screens inside dim with it |
| The haze-removal estimate, the darkest value in each neighbourhood | A pale halo with a hard rim, and the whole picture dimmed |
| Paper grown inward a fixed distance | The fade is half removed and the rim is jagged |
| Paper grown inward until the path has crossed enough paint | The pictures melt into the page with their insides intact |

The last is `paper3.py`. It takes the near-white joined to the picture's border as paper, then finds
for every other pixel the cheapest path in from that paper, where crossing a pixel costs how dark it
is. The share falls from one to nothing as that cost climbs to a budget of 3.5% of the picture's
shorter side, and is never more than the pixel's own lightness. A wide soft fade is followed all the
way in, and a dark outline stops it at once.

### Where it still fails

- **A picture cut at its edge** loses light areas near the cut: ceiling lights, a pale floor.
  `paper2.py` has a guard that mostly worked. It looks at how light the picture is just inside each
  stretch of its edge, since a fade starts white and a cut doesn't, and lets paper in only where it
  does. The guard and the path cost haven't been put together.
- **Light paint beside the fade** dims more than it should: a pale planet surface, a desk in
  lamplight. This is the guess itself going wrong, and tuning trades it against leftover halo.
- **Ink drawings** come out as black ink on a dark page. They have to stay on the route they take
  now, so something has to choose a route for each picture.

The Python takes about a third of a second a picture on the Mac. Nothing was written in Swift or
timed on a phone.

### Next

Put the edge guard into `paper3.py`, run it over every picture in both books and the earlier samples,
and look for what it breaks. Then settle the rule that tells a faded drawing from ink and from a cut
picture.
