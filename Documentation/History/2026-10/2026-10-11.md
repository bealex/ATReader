# 11 October 2026

## A finished book reopened a page before its last

Reported from the phone: read to the last page, shut the book, and after the app had been restarted
it opened on the page before. The middle of a book didn't show it.

The reader was cleared first. A book cut forward to its end and opened again at the stored offset
gives the same page, in `BookLayout` alone and through `ReaderScreen.Model` with a real store, over
generated books whose last chapter ran from one paragraph to thirty-four, in one column and two. The
bundled book read to its end on the simulator and reopened four times stood on its last page each
time.

A probe in `adoptServerPositions` wrote what the service sent beside what the device held. For the
book in question:

| | chapter progress | dated | offset |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| The device | | 1791729820.79 | 21643 |
| The service | 100% | 1791729821.94 | |

The chapter's `textLength` on the service is 20686. So the service had kept the report the reader
sent on reaching the last page, dated it 1.15 seconds after the device's own write, and the library
took it as the newer of the two on the next launch. 100% of 20686 is an offset before the 21643 the
last page starts at, so the book opened on the page that holds it.

An older row showed the same pairing from August: the service 0.3 seconds ahead of the device's
debounced write, holding the `1.0` the client's old clamp used to send. The service has been keeping
these reports all along, and `Documentation/API.md` said otherwise.

The middle of a book should show it less. The report is of the foot of the sheet and the conversion
runs short, which pull opposite ways. That part wasn't measured.

### The fix

The reader keeps what it last reported for each book, in `reported_position`, before sending it.
`LibraryScreen.Model.adopts` leaves alone an entry that matches it by chapter and percentage. Matching
on what was sent rather than on how close the two dates are means the device's clock and the
service's don't have to agree.

### Left as it was

- A position another device reported is still turned into an offset with the service's `textLength`,
  so a book finished on one device opens a page early on another.
- Positions already overwritten stay where the overwrite put them until the book is next read.

## Opening on the book the app was closed on

The reader's place already survived a restart. Which book was open didn't, so an app closed
mid-book came back on the shelf.

The book is kept in the defaults as it opens and taken back as it shuts. The first try presented
the reader from `navigationController(_:didShow:)`, which never put it up. Presenting from a task
that waits for the stack's window did.

The reader then shut itself about a third of a second after launch. Nothing in the app had called
`dismiss`: the trace showed a presentation transition starting after a commit, and a screenshot showed
the library's error alert where the page had been. A guest launch fails its first load, the library
raises "You need to sign in to do that", and SwiftUI dismisses what is over the screen to show it. A
real launch with no network would do the same. The library holds the alert while a book is open
over it.

That alert went the same day. The app reads without an account, so a load refused for want of a
session marks the shelf stale and says nothing; signing in is offered from the profile. The alert
still stands for an empty shelf that can't be loaded and for a book that can't be taken off.

Two things about the probes. `-openBook.workId -1000000` as a launch argument set nothing, the
value's minus sign being the likely reason, and `defaults write` through `simctl spawn` was what put
a kept book in front of a launch. And the preferences file in a simulator's container lags what the
app has written, so reading it said the book had never been kept.
