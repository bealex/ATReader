# 14 October 2026

## The progress bar slid in with the controls

Revealing the controls turns the page number into "page/length" with a bar beside it, through a
crossfade. The bar slid in from the left and its fill grew instead of simply fading in.

Both moves came from laying the caption out inside the transition's animation block. The bar's frame
was animated from wherever it last stood, and its fill is a bare `CALayer`, which animates a frame
change of its own accord when its view lays out. The caption now sets every frame and lays out with
animations and layer actions off, inside the block, so the transition only crossfades. A recording on
the simulator shows the bar at its full width from the first frame of the fade.

## Note markers drawn as subscripts

A note's marker came out below the line, as a subscript. So did every `<sup>` figure, and `<sub>`
figures stood above the line.

The offsets were signed for `CTLineDraw`, where the flipped text matrix made a positive
`.baselineOffset` sink and the markers took a negative one. On 7 October the page went to drawing each
run with `CTRunDraw`, so a run could take its share of the theme's ink, and there the same matrix
takes a positive offset upwards. The signs are flipped in `NoteMarker` and `ScriptMarker`.

The test that should have caught it asserted only that the offset was negative. It's replaced by two
that draw a page and compare the bottom row of the figure's ink with the bottom row of the words
before it.

## A dark page keeps a picture's subject and turns the rest over

Back to the faded pictures of 9 October, tried a different way on 128 pictures from three books on
the phone: Vision's foreground mask keeps the subject as drawn, and everything outside it is turned
over, white to black with hue kept. On line art it works; on the shaded paintings the scene behind
goes negative, which is acceptable at page size. The prototype and its sheets are in the gitignored
`Fixtures/Reports/paper-in-dark-mode/` (`tools/segsplit.swift`).

What the mask needed, in the order it was found:

- **Smoothing the outline** (closing, blurring and cutting again) rounded the jagged edge but made
  groups of figures into one blob. Dropped.
- **Eroding or growing it by a pixel or two** only moved the white outline round each figure. The
  outline is the paper between the ink and the model's edge, which falls just outside the ink.
- **Cutting back to the ink** removed it: from outside, eat into the mask through anything lighter
  than level 60 out of 255, within a band 1.5% of the picture's shorter side wide, and stop at the
  first ink. At level 180 the soft grey edge of the ink survived as a faint fringe.

In the app this is `PictureSubject`, and `PageImage.draw` uses it for every monochrome picture on a
dark page, in the page's own two colours. The model can't run in the simulator, so it was checked by
compiling `PictureSubject.swift` into a Mac tool that draws the way the reader does: 30 to 180 ms a
picture, results matching the prototype.

A picture reported off the phone (Космопсихолухи 1, i_046) lost both faces to the background. The faces
are drawn with open strokes, and the cut, eating inward through paper for 1.5% of the picture's side,
ran in through the gaps. The strip of paper the model leaves outside the ink is a couple of pixels
wide, so the band is 0.6% now, and the faces stay. The model also took the chair, the desk and both
figures as one subject and left the floating screens out, which no setting changes.

The subject is looked for in the original picture, read again from its file, at up to 4,096 pixels
on its longest side, and only the mask is scaled to the 1,600 the picture is kept at. Where the model
can't run, as in the simulator, a picture is drawn as before.

The right man's face was still turned over on the phone. A probe saved the phone's masks: the phone's
model leaves the face out, as a notch in the mask, where the Mac's includes it, so the two run
different models and the cut wasn't to blame. A step now runs before the cut: with gaps of up to two
pixels in the ink closed, any patch of paper no path from the picture's edge reaches, that stands at
least 30% within six pixels of the subject, joins it. The face comes back. A small patch inside the
curved screen beside the desk comes back with it.
