# 2026-08-23 — spacing, weight, and a green page

## 1. The spaces that would not stretch

Justified pages had wildly uneven gaps and, on bad lines, visible spreading between letters inside
words. Left-aligned pages had uneven word gaps too, which ragged-right text has no reason to.

The cause was the no-break space. `Typography` binds a Russian preposition to the word after it, and
did so with U+00A0, which is rigid: justification cannot take it up. A line with six spaces, three of
them bound, has to absorb all its slack in the other three, and when even that isn't enough TextKit
starts spreading letters. U+00A0 is also a different width from an ordinary space in most book faces,
which is why the ragged-right lines looked uneven as well.

A bound space is now an ordinary space followed by a word joiner: same width, still elastic, and no
line may break at it. Justification takes up every space on the line before it reaches for the gaps
between letters, which is the order it should have had.

**Not the fonts.** The obvious suspect was Cyrillic coverage, so every face was measured:
`CTFontCreateForString` returns the face it was asked for in all six, Charter included, and the space
advance is identical for Russian and Latin in each. Nothing falls back, so nothing mixes metrics.

| face | covers Cyrillic | draws Cyrillic with | space RU | space Latin |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Georgia | yes | Georgia | 4.58 | 4.58 |
| Palatino | yes | Palatino-Roman | 4.75 | 4.75 |
| Charter | yes | Charter-Roman | 5.28 | 5.28 |
| New York | yes | .NewYork-Regular | 4.45 | 4.45 |
| SF Rounded | yes | SF Rounded | 4.26 | 4.26 |
| SF | yes | .SFUI-Regular | 4.77 | 4.77 |

## 2. Weight, and a green page

The reader picks a weight now — light, regular, medium or semibold — and it rides in
`ChapterTextStyle` beside the face, so pagination and drawing set the same text. A named family
carries only the weights it ships and the descriptor picks the nearest; the system designs carry all
four.

A fifth page tint: dark green on near-black, for reading in the dark without a white page.
