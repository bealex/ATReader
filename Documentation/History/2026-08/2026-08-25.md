# 2026-08-25 — what the store handed back, and where the pages fall

## 1. A checkmark on a book with unread chapters

The ring on a cover means how much of the book has been read, and a tick replaces the figure at 99.5%.
Books were showing the tick with most of their chapters unopened.

`WorkMetaInfo.readingProgress` fell back to `lastChapterProgress` whenever the service gave no character
offset, and that fallback was wrong twice over. `lastChapterProgress` measures the current chapter
rather than the book, so chapter three of fifty, finished, read as a finished book. And the service
speaks `0…100`, which `min(1, max(0, …))` clamped to exactly `1.0`, so two per cent into any chapter was
enough. The scale was already recorded in `API.md` for the neighbouring fields, and
`adoptServerPositions` was already dividing `chapterProgress` by 100.

It could never recover, either. `store(works:)` upserted `reading_progress = MAX(existing, incoming)` and
`progress(_:or:)` returned `max(stored, reported)`, so one bad reading pinned a book for good. That is
why the figure re-derived from the reading position the day before didn't rescue anything: it wrote the
right lower number and `max()` put the old one straight back.

Book progress now comes from `textLengthLastRead / textLength` or is unknown, both ratchets are gone,
and the percentage conversion lives in one place that the write side goes through as well. The write
side had promised `0…1` in its doc comment while clamping to `0…100`, with callers passing fractions;
it stores nothing, so that was only ever cosmetic, but it was the same confusion.

Databases already carrying the bad figure are repaired once, on `PRAGMA user_version`: the column is
thrown away and rebuilt from the reading positions, which are the only record of what was actually read.

## 2. Blurbs that lasted until the next launch

The blurb was being stored correctly and then wiped. `store(works:)` upserted `payload =
excluded.payload`, every launch runs `replaceLibrary`, and a shelf entry becomes a `WorkSummary` with
`annotation: nil`, because the library endpoint carries no blurb.

It clobbered the other way too, which hadn't been noticed: opening a book wrote `seriesOrder: nil` and
`lastReadTime: nil` over the shelf's values, and `seriesOrder` is what orders books within a series.

`WorkSummary.merged(over:)` now fills whatever the incoming copy doesn't know from the stored one.
`readingProgress` is deliberately left out of the merge and taken as it comes, absence included: the
device's own figure lives in its own column, and carrying a stale payload figure forward would put it
beyond the reach of section 1's repair.

## 3. The chapter heading, twice

A chapter's number and title arrive from the contents, which the reader sets as a heading, and again as
the opening paragraphs of the body HTML. Both were on the page.

The body's version is dropped now. Three things the naive version got wrong:

- **A title given as one line reaches the body as two.** `Глава 7 Лабиринт каналов` in the contents,
  `Глава 7` and `Лабиринт каналов` in the body. Matching paragraph against heading found nothing, so the
  paragraphs are read together and compared to the heading as it accumulates.
- **A body that merely starts on the same word must keep it.** Each paragraph is given up only while
  everything read so far is still the opening of the heading.
- **The text carries the typesetter's marks by then.** Mapping non-alphanumerics to spaces turned the
  soft hyphen inside `Гла<shy>ва` into a space and split one word into two, so nothing matched. Format
  characters are dropped rather than spaced over. Eight cases were run through a throwaway Swift script,
  and this is the one that caught it.

## 4. Pages that moved when the reader turned back

Open chapter three from the contents and it starts a fresh page. Turn back into chapter two, which has
half a page spare, then turn forward: chapter three has climbed onto that spare half and the reader is
no longer on its first page.

A chapter that runs on is laid out shorter by however much the previous page used, so its breaks depend
on the chapter before it, whose breaks depend on the one before that. Measuring a chapter alone answers
differently from measuring it after reading in. The old `Reader.md` described this as intended: "laid
out again if the reader later reads into it from the chapter before".

`BookPagination` measures the book in one pass, in order from the first chapter, keeping only where each
chapter starts and how far it runs, and throwing each layout away as it goes. Every later layout takes
its offset from that pass. Landing on the right page needed no change once the offsets stopped moving.

Two decisions the pass forced:

- **It measures only text already on the device.** Fetching a book to find out where its pages fall
  would turn opening one chapter into a download of all of them. A chapter that isn't here ends the
  run-on and the next one starts a page of its own.
- **It runs again for every style and page size**, since that is what the measurements depend on.

Unmeasured, and the reason to watch it: the pass is a full TextKit layout of every downloaded chapter,
and marking the hyphens costs about as much again. If a long novel stalls on opening, the answer is to
keep the placements in `LocalStore` against the style that produced them, so the cost is paid once per
book instead of once per open.
