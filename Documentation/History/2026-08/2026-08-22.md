# 2026-08-22 — how a book presents itself

The library and the book page were showing everything they knew about a book, in the order the code
happened to compute it. This session was about what a reader actually looks at.

## 1. The library opens on what you are reading

The shelf filter defaulted to everything, so a library of two hundred books opened on two hundred
books. It now opens on the Reading shelf, and the filter widens it.

Books are grouped by author, and within an author by series. The grouping is a dictionary keyed by
(author, series); standalone titles come before an author's series, and books inside a series sort by
the service's `seriesOrder` before their title.

A long press moves a book between shelves or off them, which is the same `update-library-state` call
the book page already made.

## 2. Progress belongs on the cover

A linear bar under each row said the same thing three times over: a bar, a percentage, and a date.
It's now one ring on the cover itself, with the figure inside it, fixed at 30×30 whatever the cover's
size, because it's a badge rather than part of the artwork.

The "continue reading" strip lost its titles. A cover is the fastest thing to recognise, and the strip
is now sorted by when the *book* last changed rather than when the reader last opened it: the book with
a new chapter in it is the one worth picking up.

## 3. Pills

A book's facts (likes, whether it's finished, whether it's paid for, how far in you are, when it last
changed) were a mix of grey `Label`s and a page count derived by dividing characters by 1800. The count went. The rest are pills, tinted by what they say, with the glyph 3pt from its word
rather than a label's default gap.

They wrap, which SwiftUI has no stack for, so `FlowLayout` is a small `Layout` that fills a line and
starts another.

## 4. Disclosure chevrons

Every list row was a `NavigationLink`, which draws a chevron a book row doesn't need. A row is now a
button that appends to the stack's path; nothing else about navigation changed, and the UI tests that
tap cells never noticed.

## 5. The bar the system draws

The reader's bar had been hand-built to match a navigation bar, measured against one, and still read
as not quite right. It's now the navigation bar: `.toolbar(.hidden / .visible, for: .navigationBar)`
follows the same tap that used to fade a custom overlay in.

The reason the custom bar existed was that showing a real one would push the page down. That stopped
being true when the page started taking its size and insets from the window, so the change costs
nothing. The bar carries the page's colour, made visible explicitly, since the running head passes
under it.

## 6. The cover that blinked

Turning a page from the title page made the cover flash. `PageTurnView` puts the incoming page in a
different slot of its `ZStack`, so the view is rebuilt under a new identity, `@State` starts empty, and
the cover comes back through an `await` on the cache actor. One frame of placeholder, every turn.

Decoded covers now sit in a main-actor `NSCache` as well, so a view that is rebuilt draws its cover in
the first frame. The actor stays where covers are downloaded, downsampled and stored.

## 7. What the first look at it found

Reading it on a device turned up five things, all small:

- The figure in the ring is 20% smaller, and a finished book shows a tick instead of "100%".
- Book lists have no row separators. A cover and a title are their own boundary.
- The library orders by what was last read or last updated rather than by author name. Alphabetical
  order is only useful when you already know what you're looking for.
- The covers in the "continue reading" strip take the same long press as the rows.
- A refused shelf move now says what the service said, instead of "Couldn't update your library", and
  reloads the library so the shelf on screen is the one the service holds. The shelf is the service's
  own field: a book read to its end stays on Reading until something moves it.

## 8. Two more from the shelf

A series now runs latest book first, since the one with a chapter still arriving is the one being read.
Titles compare numerically, so a series the service gives no `seriesOrder` for lands in the same order
anyway.

Reading also drops a book once it's been read to the end. The shelf is still the service's field, and
the service leaves a finished book on it; the list stops showing what there is nothing left to read.
The number beside a shelf in the filter is counted through the same rule rather than taken from the
service's own totals, so it is the number of rows the shelf opens.

## Verified

Fifteen catalogue UI tests pass against the live service, including the reader's controls and page
turns. Read out of XCUITest, the reader's bar measures the same as the book page's: origin (0, 62),
402×54, back button 44×44 at x=16. That is where the system puts a navigation bar, so there is nothing
left to match by hand. Screenshots taken from a scratch XCUITest confirmed the bar's buttons, the pills and the row
without a chevron; the library screens need an account and were not seen running.
