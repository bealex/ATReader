# 2026-09-02

## Justified lines that stopped short of the measure

Reported as a ragged right edge on pages that were otherwise justified.

The binder caused it. `Typography.bound` ties a short Russian preposition to the word after it so that
no line ends on one, and the pair it makes can be wider than what's left of the line. Nothing on the
line can be broken then, so the column left it short. One reported line had a hole
of 70pt with a word of two characters waiting under it.

`freedText` already frees such a tie, but it only ran when TextKit had opened the spaces to its own
limit. A line the column had given up on filling never met that condition, so the remedy never reached
the lines that needed it. It now runs on a line left short as well. Measured on the same paragraph, the
worst hole fell from 100pt to 10pt, and that remainder is the side bearing of the letter the line
closes on.

What is left is a different case: a long word that won't hyphenate, with too few gaps to take the
slack. The column declines to open gaps past 5.2 times a space and leaves those short on purpose. Three
such lines measured 7.2, 7.2 and 11.2 times a space to fill.

## Reading where a line ends

`TypesetLine.width` reported `lineFragmentUsedRect`, which is what TextKit made of a line. The column
draws justified lines itself, run by run, so that number described something that isn't on the screen: a
line the report called full measured 54pt short in the screenshot. It reports where the ink ends now,
which is what made the defect above measurable at all.

## Wasted pages after a part title

A part title is a chapter of a heading and nothing else. `startOffset(after:)` wouldn't let anything follow a
chapter of one page, so a part title held a page to itself and the chapter after it began on
the next, leaving most of a page empty. The free space and the three-line rule already settle whether a
chapter may follow, so the page count no longer decides it.

## Lines short because the column never looked at them

The column set a line again only where TextKit was straining at it: where the first space had been
opened past 3.1 times the width the font gives one. A line TextKit left short strains at nothing, its
spaces standing at their natural width, so it failed that test and was passed over. Crowding was
standing in for "wants setting again" and excluded the lines that wanted it most.

Five readings of `loosened` said otherwise, each contradicted by a measurement. What found it was
making the column record its reason and reading that back off a device: `not crowded, so TextKit's own
setting stands`.

## Filling from the gaps and the letters together

A line wanting gaps wider than the ceiling was left short outright, however little it wanted past it.
One line asked 30.7pt a gap against the 25.3pt allowed and stood 62pt short. The gaps take what they
may now and the letters take the rest, at most a point apiece: two gaps at the ceiling carried 50pt of
that line and the letters carried the remaining 12pt at under half a point each.

## Letters before gaps, which does not work yet

Tracking reads as colour where the same space gathered into the gaps reads as holes, so the letters
should take their share first and the gaps only what is left. Written that way the layout collapses:
122 of the rendering checks fail and lines draw at a fraction of their width.

The piece a line is set in is one measure wide and holds one line, and `loosened` checks up front that
every glyph fitted. Tracking added after that check pushes the text past the measure, since kern is
added after every character including the last, so `spread x letters` slightly exceeds the slack. What
does not fit is never laid out and the runs point at glyphs that are not there.

Doing it in that order wants the fit checked again after the tracking is applied, and the spread backed
off until every glyph lays out. The old order avoided this by accident: it only ever added tracking for
what the gaps had left, which sat well inside the measure.
