# 2026-09-04

## A line pulled apart, and what it cost to leave it that way

A page off a device carried a four-word line in the middle of a paragraph, its three gaps standing at
five times the width the font gives a space.

A break was there to fix it. The line below opened on a word the dictionary breaks after its first two
letters, and drawing that fragment up would have brought the line to within a whisker of the measure
with the gaps barely open. The breaker costed that arrangement and turned it down.

## Why the arithmetic preferred the hole

The cost of an over-open line was `openGapPenalty · opened²`, where `opened` was how far the gaps had
gone into the room past the point the letters stop helping. That room runs from 2.6 spaces to 6.2, so
`opened` is a poor coordinate: the line above sat at 5.1 spaces and scored 0.71 of it, for a cost of
1.5 on top of the 1 the line already carried for reaching its comfortable ceiling.

Against 2.5 stood a hyphen at 0.35 and a second hyphen directly under the first at 1.5. The line below
already ended in one, so fixing this line cost 1.85 and saved 1.56. The breaker was right by its own
rules, and its own rules were wrong: the worst line the levers can produce scored 4, and a double
hyphen scored 1.85.

## The fix

The gaps are now priced by the space width, not by the share of the room they took:

```swift
let opened = max(0, Double(piece.fill.perGap / space) - Rules.gapStretch)
```

with `openGapPenalty` at 6. Half a space past the comfortable point costs 1.5, about what a hyphen and
its neighbour cost together; a whole space past it costs 6. `LineFill.opened` went with it.

The levers themselves didn't move: what a line *can* do is unchanged, and only what it costs to choose
one is different.

## What it did to the column

`SpacingTests` checks every line of the corpus and of running prose at 60 settings, and every reported
page, against a ceiling of 3.2 spaces. Before: 44 of the 121 columns had a line past it, the worst at
4.7. After: none. The loosest multi-gap line anywhere is 3.05, and everything else clusters at 2.6,
which is the comfortable ceiling.

Four columns still carry one line past 3.2, always the same one:

    Электрификация сельскохозяй-

Two words, one gap, and nothing to rearrange: any earlier break makes the line shorter and the gap
wider. That's why the test lets a single-gap line reach 5.

The reported page's worst line went from 5.1 to 2.5. The column broke the word ending the line above
it and sent what was left down, which is the trade the rules were meant to make all along.

## Pages reported off a device are now fixtures

`Fixtures/Reports/<name>/` takes a debug report unzipped, and `PageReport` reads its `page.txt` and
`settings.txt` into a layout context. `DevicePageTests`, `SpacingTests` and `PageRenderTests` walk them
all. `Scripts/app.sh test` names the directory in `TEST_RUNNER_AT_REPORTS`, so a report dropped there
is checked from then on with no further edit. Book text is never committed, so the directory is ignored
by git.

This replaces `AT_TEST_PAGE`, which held one page at settings written into the test by hand and went
stale the moment the reader's own settings moved.

## Traps

- **A failing app test costs ten minutes, not one.** `xcodebuild` waits the full 600 seconds on
  "Failure collecting diagnostics from simulator" after any failure. A passing run of the same suite
  takes 8 seconds. Tune against `spacing.txt` from a passing run rather than against failure output.
- **`gapMultiple` is read after the residual is shared out.** The breaker costs the arithmetic's
  answer and the page shows the shaper's, and the two differ by a fraction of a point.
