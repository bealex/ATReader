# 2026-09-20

The card that stood over a book being opened is gone.

It said "Setting the pages…" over a bar that ran on its own, and it was there because opening a book
used to measure every chapter up to the one being opened. A page is cut where the reader stopped now,
in about 50ms, so the card covered the title page for one frame and then went.

What it also covered was fetching a chapter the device hasn't got. That wait is the service's, and the
reader shows the page's own colour while it lasts; a chapter that can't be fetched at all is still a
page saying so.

## Covers were kept at print size

A cover a book brought out of a file was written to the device exactly as the file carried it. The
largest in one library measured 1500 by 2359 and took 3 MB; five hundred of them came to 257 MB, on
the device and in every backup, to draw a picture no larger than 192pt across.

Covers are held to one size now, `CoverCache.maximumPixelSize`. The tallest a cover is drawn is on a
book's own page, 192pt across and about 290pt down, which a 3x screen asks 870 pixels for. Measured over
three covers from that library, keeping the longest edge at 480 pixels costs 45 KB, at 720 costs 89 KB
and at 900 costs 128 KB; 720 is the size chosen, an eighth short of what the densest screen could use
and a thirtieth of what was being kept.

The service's own covers were already downsampled on the way in, at 480. They take the same size now,
so a book page is drawn from the picture rather than from a picture stretched half as far again.

`Covers.shrinkWhatWasKept()` brings what is already on the device down, once, behind whatever the reader
is doing, and writes the size it held them to in defaults so it runs again only if that number grows. A
picture's size is read from its header rather than by decoding it, so a library whose covers are already
small costs one pass over a directory listing.
