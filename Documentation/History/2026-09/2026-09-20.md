# 2026-09-20

The card that stood over a book being opened is gone.

It said "Setting the pages…" over a bar that ran on its own, and it was there because opening a book
used to measure every chapter up to the one being opened. A page is cut where the reader stopped now,
in about 50ms, so the card covered the title page for one frame and then went.

What it also covered was fetching a chapter the device hasn't got. That wait is the service's, and the
reader shows the page's own colour while it lasts; a chapter that can't be fetched at all is still a
page saying so.

## Covers were kept at print size

A cover a book brought out of a file was written to the device exactly as the file carried it. The
largest in one library measured 1500 by 2359 and took 3 MB; five hundred of them came to 257 MB, on
the device and in every backup, to draw a picture no larger than 192pt across.

Covers are held to one size now, `CoverCache.maximumPixelSize`. The tallest a cover is drawn is on a
book's own page, 192pt across and about 290pt down, which a 3x screen asks 870 pixels for. Measured over
three covers from that library, keeping the longest edge at 480 pixels costs 45 KB, at 720 costs 89 KB
and at 900 costs 128 KB; 720 is the size chosen, an eighth short of what the densest screen could use
and a thirtieth of what was being kept.

The service's own covers were already downsampled on the way in, at 480. They take the same size now,
so a book page is drawn from the picture rather than from a picture stretched half as far again.

`KeptPictures.shrink()` brings what is already on the device down, once, behind whatever the reader
is doing, and writes the size it held them to in defaults so it runs again only if that number grows. A
picture's size is read from its header rather than by decoding it, so a library whose covers are already
small costs one pass over a directory listing.

### The pictures inside the books

Measured over sixty books from the same library, all FB2, carrying 181 pictures between them: the
longest edge is 818 pixels at the median and 1573 at the ninth decile, 66 of them run past 1000 pixels
and 18 past 1600. Two thirds are PNG. The bytes are 43 MB for those sixty books, about 730 KB a book,
and the largest single picture is a 1500 by 2359 cover carried inside the book at 3 MB.

So the tail is what costs: holding every picture to 1600 pixels leaves 163 of the 181 exactly as they
came and takes the sixty books from 43 MB to 30 MB, which over a library of five hundred is 372 MB
down to 258 MB. 1600 is what `BookImages` already decodes a picture to, so nothing on a page changes.

A picture is written back in the format it came in. Two thirds being PNG, and a good share of those
being line work, writing them out as photographs would ring around every stroke to save a few
kilobytes.

## A mark now keeps the page it was made on

Opening a bookmark cut a page beginning at the mark, so the page that came back was never the page
the reader had marked: the marked line stood at the top of a fresh page with everything above it gone.

A mark keeps two more numbers now, the first character of its page and which line of that page it
stands on. Opened, the book cuts from that character, which reproduces the page exactly at the same
size and face. Where the book has been read again since and the words have moved, the opening moves
with them by the same distance the search found.

Marks written before this are filled in by the reader. `BookLayout.pagePlaces(of:in:)` cuts a chapter
from its own beginning until it reaches the page holding the mark, which is the only way to learn
where a page begins when pages are cut one at a time. It runs once a book, at utility priority, after
the first page is on screen, and only for the chapters that hold an unplaced mark.

## A picture stood too close to the text above it

Reported from a page where the picture began directly under the last line. Measured off that screenshot,
at three pixels to the point: nineteen pixels between the text's ink and the top of the picture, against
thirty between the ink of two consecutive lines.

The air around a picture was `style.lineSpacing * 1.62`, split above and below, and the reader on that
page had set the line spacing to about two points. So the picture stood 3pt clear inside its own line,
and about 6pt from the ink above it: a quarter of what falls between two lines of the same text, which
is why it read as touching. Measured again in a layout at 19pt with 7pt of line spacing, the gap above
came to 18pt, which is why nobody had noticed it at the default setting.

The air is a line of the face now, `style.font.lineHeight`, above and below. A full line of the page
read a shade loose, so it is the face's own line height rather than the page's pitch: 30pt above the
picture and 23pt below at that setting, against 16pt between two lines of ordinary text, and it holds
its proportions at every line spacing rather than vanishing at a tight one.

## The page number counts itself as the reader turns

The number at the foot of a page is the book's length in pages times how far into the book the page
ends, and that length came from a count of characters. An estimate is a page or two out over a
chapter, so a reader turning forward saw the same number twice, or saw one skipped.

Every turn forward corrects the length now. The turn is a measurement: the page just left was showing
a number and the page arrived on has to show the next. `ReaderScreen.Model.pages(holding:at:reaching:at:from:)`
takes each place's window of lengths, `(n - 0.5) / share` up to `(n + 0.5) / share`, and picks the
length nearest the one in hand that lies in both.

The windows often do not meet. At a step of two tenths of a percent no whole length numbers 0.500 as
50 and 0.502 as 51: the first asks for 99 or 100, the second for 101 or 102. The page in front of the
reader wins there, since the page behind has already gone off the screen.

Turning back corrects nothing, those pages having been numbered on the way out, and a fresh estimate
from a chapter arriving or a page of another size puts the count back where `BookLength` had it.
