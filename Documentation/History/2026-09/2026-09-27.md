# 2026-09-27

## Control Center closed the book

Swiping down from the top edge to open Control Center closed the reader. The swipe is a downward
drag on the page, so the zoom back to the cover began; the system then took the touch, and UIKit's
zoom transition finishes a dismissal whose touch is cancelled rather than going back. There's no
public way to reverse one once it has begun.

So the dismissal never begins for a drag that started in the system's band. Each zoomed screen
carries a `TouchDownRecognizer` that records whether the latest touch came down above the window's top
safe-area inset and fails straight away, and `interactiveDismissShouldBegin` refuses the drag when it
did. A drag from anywhere lower still closes the book. The simulator can't make the system's edge
swipe, so this was checked on a device.

## A book opened from its cover opens

Tapping a cover used to zoom the reader out of it. Now the shut book is lifted to the middle of the
screen, the cover swings open about the spine, curling as it goes, and the page grows to fill the
screen; shutting is the same run backwards. A spine, a cover partly off the screen and Reduce Motion
still get the zoom. UIKit's zoom can't be told to draw anything of its own, so the opening is a
transitioning delegate of its own with its own drag to shut, driven frame by frame from one number.

The cover is 24 flat strips from the hinge out. The free edge leads the hinge by up to π/3.5 halfway
over, gathered towards the edge with a power of 1.6, so the board lies flat at both ends and curls
between. Perspective is baked into each strip's transform around the middle of the screen, three
lifted-book widths away, and the strips are ordered by how far towards the eye each one stands.

Four things turned up on the way:

- **A blank board is see-through.** A cover with no artwork printed yet is drawn in the system's fill
  colour, which is translucent. The shelf's version reads dark because of the bookcase behind it; flown
  over the page, the page's text showed through it. The picture is now laid over a render of the screen
  underneath at the cover's rect, taken with the stand-in hidden.
- **A drag is read in the window's space.** The page is scaled down as it shuts, so a translation read
  in its own coordinates would grow as the book shrinks.
- **A touch during the opening goes unheard.** The photo test dragged the book shut two seconds after
  tapping it, with motion slowed eightfold, which is still inside the opening. The frames of the opening
  finishing read like the book springing back open; the drag's handler was never called.
- **`testADragDownThePageClosesTheBook` couldn't fail.** It waited for the library list, which stays in
  the window under an over-full-screen reader. It waits for the page to go now.

## The opening, seen on a device

A recording off the phone showed three things wrong.

- **The library's bars showed through the page.** The glass buttons and the tab bar sat over the page
  for the whole flight and went the moment the reader arrived. It looked like the glass being drawn
  above the transition, and it isn't: an opaque view in the transition container covers the bars just
  as one in a window of its own does. The reader is see-through wherever it draws nothing, and
  `ReaderBarAppearance` paints the stack and window behind it in the page's colour only in
  `viewDidAppear`, giving them back in `viewWillDisappear`. So for the length of any transition the
  screen underneath shows through. The book now carries a sheet of the reader's paper behind the page.
- **The page was cropped.** It was scaled to fill the book, which is shorter for its width than the
  screen, so its top and bottom were cut off. It's scaled to fit now, letterboxed on that sheet, so the
  book shows the page as it will be laid out.
- **The reader blinked once the book had shut.** The run put the page back to full size before handing
  over to UIKit, which removes it a frame later. The page is taken out of the container first now.

## Easing the cover, and a drag that answers

The cover opens over the first quarter of the run, while the book is still being lifted, and shuts
over the last quarter of a close. A cubic ease-in over most of the run, slow at first and fast at the
end, was tried first and didn't read well on the device. The drag to shut felt dead at first because every phase was a
smoothstep, flat at both ends, and the page's growth ended flat exactly where a drag starts. The
gentle start and stop moved onto the clock instead (smoothstep over time for a full run, a cubic
ease-out for a run a finger let go of), and the growth became an ease-in that is still moving at full
size. A drag now shuts the book over 40% of the screen's height rather than 60%, and a release past a
quarter of that shuts it.

## One sweep

The book used to travel in two moves: lifted to the middle of the screen at the cover's own shape, then
grown from there to the screen's. It reads as a stop halfway. The book's rectangle now runs straight
from the cover to the screen on `open²`, slow off the shelf and fastest as it fills the screen, and the
clock keeps to time for a full run so that curve is what shows. The cover still opens over the first
quarter, while the book is small, so the eye it's projected from moved from the middle of the screen to
straight in front of the book: seen from the middle of the screen, a book at the shelf's edge turned
at a slant.

The cover then took longer to open, settling at the first 75% of the run rather than a quarter.

The book's centre then left the straight line. Across it takes a smoothstep; down it follows a quartic
Bézier with control values 0, 0, 1.7, 1, 1, which leaves and arrives at rest and runs about 8% of the
way past the middle, near three-quarters of the run, before settling. The size keeps `open²`.

## Flying low, and a book in hand

The overshoot read well from the top shelf and badly from the bottom one, where the book rose past the
middle and came back down. The path now dips instead: the size and the horizontal move share one
smoothstep, and the vertical adds `0.15 × screen height × (27/4) t² (1 − t)` below the straight line,
deepest two-thirds of the way. From the top shelf the book sinks past the middle and rises back; from the
bottom it stays low and rises into place.

A drag used to run the animation backwards by the finger's travel. Now the book's middle is pinned under
the finger, and the travel down sets only its size: smoothstep is flat at full size, so the travel is
turned into `open` through the smoothstep's inverse, `½ − sin(asin(1 − 2g) / 3)`, and the book shrinks
from the first point dragged. On release the shift off the path runs to nothing along a cubic Hermite
whose starting tangent is the finger's velocity, so a flick carries the book on before it turns home.

The horizontal move then took an overshoot back: the quartic Bézier with control values 0, 0, 1.5, 1, 1,
which runs about 5% of its travel past the middle before settling. A book standing near the middle of
the screen barely moves across, so it barely overshoots either.

The cover still opened and shut too quickly: with the free edge leading, it stood edge-on about 0.32 s
into the run. It now takes 85% of the run and follows the smoothstep of `u^1.5` rather than of `u`, so it
lifts slowly off the page and stands edge-on at about 0.46 s, still settling gently at either end.

The end of the opening still stopped dead. The dip, `t² (1 − t)`, was rising at full speed on arrival,
so the book's last move ended in a stop. The dip is now `t³ (1 − t)²`, deepest at three-fifths and
arriving at rest, and the size and the move across and down take smootherstep rather than smoothstep,
so they arrive without acceleration either. The drag's inverse of the size curve is now a bisection.

## The jump at the start

The book jumped as it left the shelf. Logging the clock's ticks on the simulator at full speed showed
the first frame arriving 90 ms after the run began, 10.6% of the way in, and another 48 ms stall two
frames later: the reader lays itself out for the first time as the transition adds it, on the main
thread. The clock counted that time, so the first tenth of the flight was never drawn. It now counts
from the first frame it draws, and a frame that runs long moves it on by no more than 1/30 s. Measured
again, the first tick is at 0 and the stalls step 33 ms each. The book's drop shadow also appeared at
full strength on the first frame, where the shelf had none; it fades in over the first 15% now.

Both overshoots came down: the horizontal Bézier's middle control value from 1.5 to 1.25, which runs
about 1.3% past the middle instead of 5.5%, and the dip from 15% of the screen's height to 10%.
