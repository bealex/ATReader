# 2026-09-08

## Footnotes were being thrown away twice over

A book's notes reached the reader as stray digits. `BookHTML` stripped every tag with one regex, so an
`<a href="#n1">1</a>" left its `1` glued to the word before it and nothing else. The note itself went
two different ways depending on where the book came from: a chapter from the service kept the note's
block and set it as a paragraph at the foot of the chapter, and a file lost it outright, since the
parser skipped every body after the first. There was a comment in `FB2Parser` saying so.

## One shape, two sources

The service anchors a chapter into itself and keeps the note under a matching `id`. A file keeps its
notes in a body of their own at the end, far past the chapter referring to them. Rather than teach the
reader both, the parser now carries each note down to the chapters whose anchors name it and writes it
out as the same markup the service sends. Everything downstream reads one shape, which is the same
bargain `ParsedBook.Section` already made by carrying service markup rather than a format of its own.

## The marker keeps its characters

The obvious move is to renumber the markers and set them as clean superscript figures. That would move
every reading position in every book on the device, because a position is an offset into this text and
`[12]` is four characters where `12` is two. So the characters stay exactly as the text gave them and
only their setting changes: smaller, raised, and carrying the note's id on the glyph run.

The raise is 0.25 of the type size at 0.65 of its size, short of what a printed superscript takes.
`ColumnComposer` reads its line height and its baseline off the body font, so a marker climbing past
the body's own ascent would foul the line above it rather than making room for itself.

Marks travel as offsets into the text as it arrived rather than as it was set. Binding and hyphenation
both insert format characters, so a mark counted straight through drifts a little further with every
word joiner and soft hyphen ahead of it. Counting only the characters the text came with is what the
stored reading position already does.

## What made the flattening safe

The text a paragraph ends up with has to come out byte for byte the same as before, or every stored
position shifts. Collapsing white space and decoding entities both move a position, so a marker is
fenced with two private-use characters before the flattening and read back out afterwards. Neither
step touches them, no book contains one, and they are gone from the text that is stored.

## Measuring a smaller run

`ParagraphRuler` takes a single font for the paragraph, off its first character, and uses it for the
space width, the line height and the ascender. That looked like it would measure a smaller marker at
the body size and leave the line loose. It doesn't: the offsets that decide where a line breaks come
from `CTLineCreateWithAttributedString` over the real attributed text, runs and all, so a marker set
smaller measures smaller. The single font only ever decides paragraph-level metrics, which a marker
doesn't change.

## Reaching a marker with a finger

A superscript digit is two or three points across and it stands inside the third of the page that turns
it, so the turning zone would swallow every tap meant for one. The page is now asked about a note
before the zones see the tap, and a marker's target is widened to a finger's width about its own
middle, which makes it findable without moving it.

The tap resolves the way the page draws: the same walk down the lines with the same cursor, then the
marker's own glyph run, which carries the id. Reading the attribute off the run avoids counting
characters back through the soft hyphens the line was set with.

## What is deliberately not a note

A link out of the book is left as the text it always was. So is an anchor pointing at something the
chapter doesn't carry: an empty popup is worse than a digit. And a block nothing points at stays in the
chapter, because a note nobody refers to is a paragraph the reader would otherwise silently lose.

## Where this is still a guess

The service's own footnote markup was not available to check against, so the reader was built for the
shape these things generally take: a `#` anchor with the note under a matching id, or an anchor
carrying its words on a `title` attribute. FB2 is from the spec and is certain. If a service chapter
turns out to mark its notes some third way, the reader is one rule wider rather than a rewrite.

## Three things the first pass got wrong

**Nothing re-ran the reader.** Notes were read correctly and never appeared, because prepared chapter
text is kept against a hash that folds in `Typography.version`, and the source of a chapter hadn't
moved. The version is exactly the switch for "the rules changed but the source didn't", and it wasn't
bumped. Every chapter on the device was still serving paragraphs made by a typesetter that had never
heard of a note.

**The marker sank instead of rising.** The page draws through a flipped text matrix, so CoreText's own
upwards is the page's downwards, and a positive `.baselineOffset` put every marker below its line. The
sign now lives in `NoteMarker.baselineOffset(forFontSize:)`, which is one place to read and one thing
to test, rather than a minus sign buried in a call site.

**The popup opened in the top corner, clipped.** Diagnosed as an `offset`, which moves what is drawn
and leaves the layout frame behind, and a popover points at the frame. That much was right. Replacing
it with `position` fixed nothing, and only a test said so: `position` hands back a view filling
everything it was offered, so the aside was still hung on the whole panel and still opened from its
corner. A popover given a corner to open into is given the room a corner has, which is where the
clipping came from. Padding is the one of the three that actually moves the frame.

## What the test had to look like

The obvious test measures where an aside opened against where it was anchored. That can't work: the
system decides whether a popover opens above or below its point, so a correctly hung aside is routinely
nowhere near it.

What discriminates is two points on **one** panel. An aside that ignores its point hangs from that
panel's corner both times and opens in exactly the same place, so the two frames coming out equal is
the bug and the two differing is the fix. It read 0.0 against `position` and passed against padding,
which is the only reason the second bug was found rather than shipped.

A first attempt scrolled for a panel that was a `ZStack` of shapes. SwiftUI makes no accessibility
element for that, so the query never resolved and the run took eleven minutes to fail. It is a button
now, and both points sit on one panel, so the catalogue is scrolled once rather than once per point.

## Picking text off a drawn page

A page drawn with CoreText has no selection, so all of one had to be built: which character a point is
over, where the word around it ends, which boxes to paint, and what the words say once the typesetter's
own marks come out. The arithmetic is the same trick the note markers use, counting past the soft
hyphens and joiners rather than through them, and it is checked as round trips through a real laid-out
chapter rather than against coordinates written into a test.

The gesture was harder than the arithmetic. SwiftUI's `LongPressGesture` carries no place, and the
zero-distance drag usually paired with it to find one swallows every tap on the page: shipping that
broke the footnote tap, the tap that shows the controls, and the page turn, all at once.
`UILongPressGestureRecognizer` reports its own place, fires while the finger is down, and can be told
not to cancel the touches around it. It hangs on the window, since that is the one view certain to see
every touch whatever SwiftUI has put in between.

Stopping the page turning took three goes, and each found a different hole: the drag, the end of the
drag, and the tap zones. A turn can also already be under way when a press takes hold, because a finger
covers eight points in less time than a press takes to be held, so taking hold now drops it.

## An aside that would not animate

Four bugs wearing one coat, found in this order:

The system's popover paints its arrow from the presentation rather than from the content, and hands
over neither. Pixels either side of the card's edge read 230 and 245 and no `presentationBackground`
moved them. So the card and its pointer became one filled path, which cannot come out two colours and
casts one shadow.

Then the aside would not animate at all in the reader while animating perfectly in the catalogue. The
card was measured before being placed and hidden until the measurement arrived, so it played its whole
arrival where nobody could see it. Placing it by alignment instead removed both the measurement and the
hiding.

That fixed the catalogue and not the reader, and the reason is worth keeping: a transition only runs
where SwiftUI counts the insertion as animatable, and the reader's tree rebuilds on every model change.
No amount of `withAnimation` at the call site helps, because the call site was never the problem.
`CalloutPresenter` holds the aside for as long as it is coming or going and animates a number instead.

Then it grew from the wrong place, because `scaleEffect(anchor:)` reads its point against the view it is
attached to and it was attached to the region around the card rather than to the card. And then it
shrank towards the screen's corner, because the caller drops the rectangle the moment an aside is
dismissed and the presenter was holding the item but not the place.

Three of those four were invisible from the code, which is the argument for moving the page surface to
UIKit: every hard thing left in the reader is about touches, coordinates and time.

## Measuring an animation

Five test runs measured nothing because `XCUIScreenshot` takes longer to make than a 0.34 second
animation runs for: every frame caught was the last one. `-at-motion-scale` stretches the timings for a
test, and each frame is compared against a photograph of the bare page rather than matched on a colour,
since the card's ground is a shade of the page and matching on it finds the whole catalogue.

What that bought: the arrival now reads as a climb from a speck at the anchor to full size over sixteen
frames, where the same measurement before the fix showed the first frame already settled. The bounce is
the one thing those frames do not prove — the overshoot measured 0.6 points against a sampling step of
0.67 — so it is asked for explicitly rather than left to the spring's default.
