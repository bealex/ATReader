# 2026-09-29

## The opening cover is shaded smoothly

The back of the cover showed as a row of grey bands. Each strip of the curl had one flat shade, taken at
its middle, so neighbours stepped from one tone to the next. Each strip now carries a gradient from the
curl's angle at its inner edge to its angle at its outer one, and strips no longer antialias the edges
they share, which had let the page show through as a hairline at every seam. More strips only made the
bands narrower, so the cover is still cut into 24.

## Profiling the opening

Instruments couldn't record on the phone. `xctrace` either timed out "waiting for device to boot" or
found no process by name or by pid, and `log collect` from the device needs root. The measurements
are from the iPhone 17 simulator at 60 Hz: a UI test opening and shutting the bundled book six times
at full speed (four by the close button, two by a drag), timed by a probe in `Run.ticked` and profiled
by attaching Time Profiler to the app's pid, which is an ordinary Mac process there.

Nearly all the cost was the reader laying itself out. Setting `transform` on the reader's
`_UIHostingView` notifies UIKit's geometry observers, and SwiftUI updated its graph and ran
`layoutSubviews` on every frame: 338 of the 768 main-thread samples inside `OpeningBook.draw`. The
strips and their gradients were 41.

| per frame, `draw(open:shift:)` | before     | after       |
|--------------------------------|------------|-------------|
| mean                           | 0.5–1.9 ms | 0.2–0.6 ms  |
| worst                          | 3.7 ms     | 1.4 ms      |
| samples in the frame callback  | 828        | 303         |

Moving the transform to the layer broke the reader: SwiftUI had laid the page out while it stood small
over the cover and never heard it had grown, so a tap in the middle of the page missed. Taking the book
away now sets the view's transform once. The shade colours are made as `CGColor` directly and the paper
sheet is sized through its layer, which dropped a colour-space conversion and a safe-area pass per frame.

## The reader moves to UIKit

After the frames themselves were cheap, an opening still waited about 26 ms for its first frame, and
about 100 ms the first time after launch. Time Profiler put that wait in SwiftUI building the reader's
view graph when the screen arrived: type metadata and conformance lookups, preference combiners, the
navigation stack, toolbar appearance and glass. The pages themselves were about a sixth of it, and the
first opening paid for the runtime's first lookups on top.

The reader is now a UIKit `ReaderScreen.Controller` laid out by hand, with `PageTurner` in place of
`PageTurnView` and `TitlePageView` in place of `BookTitlePageView`. The controls, the asides and the
forms stay SwiftUI in a layer built the first time any of it is wanted. `ProgressBarView` is the UIKit
side of `ProgressBar`. Screens follow the model through `updateProperties()`, so the Info.plist sets
`UIObservationTrackingEnabled`.

Every redraw also asked where each bookmark stands by searching its chapter for the mark's words, about
2 ms an opening on the main thread. The model keeps each place it finds.

| wait for an opening's first frame | SwiftUI  | UIKit   |
|-----------------------------------|----------|---------|
| first after launch                | ~117 ms  | ~31 ms  |
| every one after                   | ~27 ms   | ~27 ms  |
| main thread busy in that wait     | ~15 ms   | ~4.5 ms |

The steady wait didn't move because the main thread no longer fills it. A run-loop observer shows the
thread idle 4.5 ms after the transition starts, waking only for fractions of a millisecond, yet the
display link's first tick comes a refresh late, at 26 ms against 17. Shutting, which adds nothing to the
screen, ticks at the first refresh, around 11 ms.

A second profile, of every process on the simulator with signposts at the transition's start, its first
frame and the reader's updates, found where that frame went. The tap's own update cycle runs from about
3 ms before the transition to 6 ms after it and commits in time. UIKit then runs a whole update cycle at
the next refresh, and the opening's `CADisplayLink`, added partway through the tap's cycle, doesn't fire
in it. It fires on the refresh after. Shutting added its link early enough in its cycle to catch the
next one. Driven by a `UIUpdateLink`, which runs inside UIKit's update cycle, the first frame of every
opening lands on the next refresh: about 13 ms after the transition starts, against 27 to 30, and 16 ms
for the first opening after launch.

## On the phone, at 120 Hz

Instruments still couldn't reach the iPhone 17 Pro Max, and `app.sh test` runs UI tests on a simulator
whatever it's told, so a probe in the app timed each run and wrote a line to its Documents, fetched with
`devicectl device copy from`. The openings were real taps.

The opening ran at 48 fps and the shutting at 60 on a 120 Hz screen. An iPhone holds an app to 60 Hz
until its Info.plist sets `CADisableMinimumFrameDurationOnPhone`, and a frame clock then has to ask for
more; with both, a run is 103 frames at 8.3 ms, with at most one late.

What's left, in milliseconds on the phone:

| opening, from the tap                  |     |
|----------------------------------------|-----|
| building the screen                    | 0.5 |
| presentation, to `viewDidLoad`         | 4.5 |
| the transition's own setup             | 2.5 |
| `backed`, the library behind the cover | 5–7 |
| the reader's first update              | 3   |
| first frame                            | ~24 |

Shutting spends 7–8.5 ms in `backed`, then a second 18.5 ms stretch the probe had no mark in, and moves
at about 47 ms. `backed` draws the whole library for a board that only needs it while its artwork isn't
printed, which is almost never by the time a reader opens it.

The cover is now backed only while its board is see-through. That took the transition's own setup
from about 13 ms to 5.5 when shutting and from 15 to 9.5 when opening.

The stretch at the start of shutting is the library rebuilding its whole screen, about 21 ms. The
reading position is written as the book starts to close, and the write ends in
`BookInbox.libraryChanged()`, so the shelf's marks are current before the zoom takes its picture. A
cover that opens takes its picture in the transition's setup, before that reload lands, so here it buys
nothing. Even with the main thread idle by 32 ms, the first frame of shutting then comes at 50 to 97 ms.

The reader's first update costs 3.4 ms. Its call into the model's layout is 0.3 ms of that, so putting
the call off a turn saved nothing and was taken back out.

A cover that opens now writes the position without telling the library, which reads the store again
once the book has landed. The spine's zoom still tells it at once. The 21 ms rebuild is gone from the
start of shutting. Many openings then moved one frame after the tap, at 8.2 ms. The rest took 24 ms:
the tap took 8 ms to reach the transition, and the status bar's update cost 3.4 ms instead of 1.2. Building
the notes' VoiceOver actions costs 1.5 to 3.5 ms while the book is still in flight.

Shutting still waits 50, 66 or 82 ms for its first frame, whole 16.7 ms steps, with the main thread idle
but for 1.5 ms of work every 16.7 ms. Something animates at 60 Hz for those frames, and the book waits
for it. A `CADisplayLink` waited the same, so the clock isn't the cause; the probe can't see further.

Attaching Time Profiler at the moment the app launched froze it twice, and a frozen recorder ignores
`SIGTERM`: the next recording hangs behind it until it's killed with `-9`.

## Tests that fail without this change

Run against `HEAD` in a worktree, these fail the same way: the two `LoginUITests` ("sign-in screen
never appeared"), `ReaderPlaceUITests.testPositionSurvivesBeingBackgroundedAndKilled` (the text comes
back, the page count moves from 3 of 148 to 4 of 159), `ReaderSearchUITests` (the bar 93.3 points over
the keys, with the keyboard's frame reaching below the screen), `SpineMenuUITests` on a spine ("the
shelf stood no book on its edge"), and `DevicePageTests` against the local reports (each 11 points
short of its recorded height). `BookmarkUITests` failed once on a mark in the licence chapter left by an
earlier run, which its `firstMatch` found after the mark it made was gone.
