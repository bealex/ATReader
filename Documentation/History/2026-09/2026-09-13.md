# 2026-09-13

Reading EPUB, and what the reader had to learn to show one: emphasis, lists, and books written from
the right. Zip64 came along with it.

## What the format gave away for free

The spine and the navigation between them state what `FB2Parser` infers. One document is one chapter,
in the order the spine lists, named by the navigation and standing at the depth it gives. `ParsedBook`
already carried a `level` that nothing had ever filled in meaningfully; this fills it.

Three-quarters of the integration was already there. `BookInstaller`, `LocalBookFiles`, the numbering,
the deduplication, re-import, the prepared-text and measurement caches are all format-neutral, and
`BookImporting.formats` was already an array. `ZipArchive` needed a way to fetch a member by name and
nothing else structural.

## Where the work actually was

`EpubContent`, and it was all of it. Three things went wrong before it read a real book, and none of
them showed up against a file written to match the specification.

**`XMLParser` could not open any of the four books.** Every one of them names entities that only the
HTML DTD ever declared, and one undeclared entity fails the whole document. Rewriting the reduction on
top of a scanner cost more than the rest of the format put together.

**`<meta>` is void in HTML and not in an EPUB package.** EPUB 3 states half of what it knows as the
text inside a `<meta>`. Treating it as void, which the HTML element list says to, silently swallowed
`rendition:layout` and every refinement, so fixed-layout books read as ordinary ones. The test caught
it; nothing else would have.

**`"#n1".split(separator: "#")` gives one piece, not two.** Empty subsequences are dropped by default,
so every footnote marker pointing inside its own document was read as an ordinary link and thrown away.

## Chaptering, three attempts

1. **Cut at headings.** Wrong, and worse, quietly wrong: the level was read off the `h` rather than the
   digit after it, so the heading list came back empty and every document stayed whole. Project
   Gutenberg puts fourteen chapters in one file, so *A Journey to the Centre of the Earth* came out as
   four chapters of 130,000 characters.
2. **Cut at the shallowest heading level.** Better, but a file opening with the book's title in an
   `<h1>` and its chapters in `<h2>` cut into two pieces: the title, and everything else.
3. **Cut where the navigation says.** Right, and it is the format's own answer. `EpubContent` marks the
   blocks the navigation's anchors name, `EpubSections` cuts there, and headings are only the fallback
   for a document the navigation names once or not at all.

Two rules had to be added on top, both from looking at real files:

- Gutenberg lists a chapter's number and its title as two navigation entries, so cutting there gives
  alternating stubs and bodies. A stub is folded into the chapter it heads and both names are kept.
  The first version of this rule folded any piece under 60 characters, which swallowed short real
  chapters; a stub is now a piece under 60 characters **with no paragraph in it**, which is what a
  heading standing alone actually looks like.
- A contents page is a page whose lines are mostly titles the navigation also lists. Testing the
  structure rather than the page's name means it holds in any language.

## Measurements

Four Project Gutenberg books, read on the Mac in the package tests:

| book | chapters | characters | pictures |
|---|---|---|---|
| *A Journey to the Centre of the Earth* | 51 | 498,433 | 4 |
| *Dracula* | 36 | 851,999 | 2 |
| *The Odyssey* | 32 | 695,573 | 1 |
| *Time Crime* | 8 | 230,515 | 15 |

All four read in about 0.9 seconds together, which is the whole suite rather than one book.

*Time Crime* comes out as 8 chapters because its navigation has five entries: it is a magazine serial,
and the file genuinely says nothing about where its scenes divide. That is the source's limit, not the
reader's.

## What the reader learned

- **Emphasis.** `BookHTML` already fenced `<sub>` and `<sup>` with private-use characters so their
  places survived the flattening. The same machinery now carries `<em>` and `<strong>` as `StyleMark`s,
  with a depth count per kind so a stretch nested inside another of its own kind closes once.
  `ChapterPagination` adds the trait to whatever face already stands there, so a phrase set both ways
  comes out as both.
- **Lists.** The item's mark goes into its own text rather than beside it, because a reading position
  counts the characters of a chapter and anything the page shows has to be among them. Only the indent
  is left to say, and `ParagraphRuler` gained `headIndent` for it: until now only a first line was ever
  indented.
- **Right to left.** The direction rides in the markup as `dir="rtl"` per block, so a chapter body
  still holds everything needed to set it. `ParagraphRuler` has to put the direction back on the text
  it measures, because the paragraph style is stripped before CoreText sees it and without it a line
  opening on a Latin word inside an Arabic paragraph is set the wrong way round. `PageTurnView` mirrors
  every horizontal distance it reads or draws, which turned out to be four places.

`Typography.version` and `ChapterLayout.rulesVersion` are both bumped: the prepared text carries marks
it did not before, and where a line stands has changed.

## Two UI tests that were already failing

`BookmarkUITests` and `ReaderControlsUITests.testMiddleTapTogglesTheControls` were failing before any
of this. Stashing the whole day's work and running them against the unmodified tree gave the same four
assertions at the same lines, which is what settled it.

Both came from one thing, and it was a real defect rather than a test artefact.

**Faded out is not gone.** The reader's controls were hidden with `.opacity(0)` and
`.allowsHitTesting(false)`. A view at no opacity is still in the accessibility tree: VoiceOver would
offer the reader buttons that are not on screen, and a test looking for `Appearance` found it. The
diagnostic that settled it printed `exists=true hittable=false`, which says exactly that the view was
correctly inert and still findable.

`.accessibilityHidden(isChromeHidden)` did not fix it. `GlassRow` puts its buttons under
`.glassEffect`, and the flag does not reach through. The controls are now built only while they are up,
with the fade kept as a transition.

**A button that does nothing.** With the controls reachable again, `BookmarkUITests` still failed: a
book opens on its title page, that page carries none of a chapter's text, so `displayedRanges` is empty
and `toggleBookmark()` makes no mark. The reader was offering an `Add bookmark` button that did
nothing. It is now disabled where no mark can stand, and the test steps past the title page the way
`CatalogUITestCase.turnPage` already says reading tests must.

A run of the two suites took 745 seconds while failing and 137 seconds once fixed. Nearly all of that
was XCUITest waiting out timeouts on queries that could never match.

## Warnings and dead code

Six compiler warnings went: two unused `UIView?` results from the zoom source, two `guard let layout`
bindings nothing read, a `weak` capture that differed from the strong one around it, and a `bind`
overload that had lost its `@discardableResult`.

One dead declaration went with them: `ProfileScreen.Component` read `BookInbox` out of the environment
and never used it. `BookholdApp.appDelegate` looks equally unused and is not: it is how SwiftUI
installs an app delegate at all.

## Reading a book the way its own publisher set it

Four things, all of them visible on the first EPUB anyone opened.

**Paragraphs now follow the tradition of the language.** Russian indents the first line and leaves no
gap; English parts its paragraphs with space and indents nothing. Before this every book got the
indent, whatever it was written in.

**Whose heading the page shows is settled by what the body wrote it as.** The Odyssey came out with
`CHAPTER 6 / THE ODYSSEY. BOOK I` above a body that opened by repeating both lines, because Project
Gutenberg names a chapter's number and its title as two navigation anchors and the stub-folding put
them together. The existing dropper only matched the number and the title read as one string, so it
caught nothing here. Now the opening blocks are matched against the title alone as well, and where the
book wrote them as headings the reader's own heading goes instead of theirs. Titles standing in the
body had no face of their own until now, which is why they read as centred body text.

**A book's own contents page is dropped.** Dracula's survived the first rule, since its lines read
`CHAPTER I. Jonathan Harker's Journal` where the navigation says `CHAPTER I JONATHAN HARKER'S
JOURNAL`. The rule that catches it is structural: most of a contents page's words stand inside links
into the book, and no chapter reads like that.

**Links work.** A `<a href>` into the book becomes a `LinkMark` on the paragraph and a `data-anchor` on
the block it lands on, so a book's own index is navigable. Where a link lands is only known after the
whole book has been read, so the addresses are scanned out of the markup in a pass before anything is
reduced.

That turned up a bug in the note handling: every short block standing under an id was remembered as a
possible note, so a link's target was being carried into the chapter that linked to it and shown as a
note. Notes are now limited to the blocks something pointed at as a note.

Counts across the four books on hand, after all of it:

| book | chapters | links | anchors |
|---|---|---|---|
| *A Journey to the Centre of the Earth* | 51 | 10 | 53 |
| *Dracula* | 35 | 0 | 27 |
| *The Odyssey* | 32 | 374 | 368 |
| *Time Crime* | 8 | 0 | 0 |

Dracula's links fall to nothing because the page holding them was its contents.

## What an FB2 opens with

A reported book was missing the two epigraphs standing between its title page and chapter one. They're
not in a section: FB2 lets a `<body>` carry a picture, a title and any number of epigraphs before its
first `<section>`, and the parser had nowhere to put them. Text lands in the innermost open section,
and before the first section there was none, so all of it fell out.

Across the 125 files on hand, 77 carry a body title, 29 a body picture and 15 body epigraphs.

The body is now opened the way a part is, which is machinery the parser already had: a section holding
sections emits what it holds directly as a page of its own as soon as it gains a child. A body is that
same shape, so this is one `Open` pushed when the body starts and the existing rules do the rest.

Two things don't come across. A body's `<title>` names the book rather than a chapter (75 of the 77 are
the author's name and then the book's, a title page set as markup), and the reader shows that before
the first page anyway. And a picture the body opens with that is the plate the description already
named as the cover is the cover a second time, which 8 of the 29 were.

## Putting a book away, twice wrong

Putting a book away in the middle of a long run showed up two faults at once, neither of them in the
turn itself. `ShelfPutAwayTests` and `ShelfStepTests` hold both, and a specimen on the catalogue's
motion page puts one book away on a run of fourteen so either can be watched rather than reasoned about.

**Books flew across the bookcase.** A cover turning onto its edge gives back most of its width, so the
books after it close up and the ones that no longer fit their row move to the row above. `stand()`
carried every book from where it stood to where it was going by interpolating the rectangle, which for
a book changing rows is a straight line over the plank and through the books it is joining. Photographed
at eight times slow, three spines hang in mid-air over the shelf, drawn on top of the bracket and the
row they are joining.

A book that changes rows is now re-shelved rather than carried: it leaves the row it was on in the first
frame, so the books closing up have the room it left, and comes in at its new place. A ghost fading out
on the old row was tried first and was worse, because the books closing up run straight over it.

**The card stepped.** `LibraryList` applied its snapshot before starting any turn, and applying lays
the list out: in that pass the card was given the height it was going to, all at once, and the books
then animated inside a card that had already arrived. Measured through the collection view, an eleven
book card went from 360 to 183 points in the frame it was told, and the fold turn was no better, 183 to
714. The turns now start first and the snapshot is applied after, and the card travels: 360, 273, 210,
189, 184, 183.

**The menu was still putting the book back.** A book is lifted for its menu as a `UITargetedPreview`
over the view itself, and UIKit returns that preview to the view's own centre, over its own time. The
deed fired as the menu began closing, so the shelf started turning the same book while UIKit was still
carrying it home: the cover shrank towards the middle of where it had been rather than onto the edge its
spine now stood on. `BookView.whenMenuCloses` holds the work until the interaction's own animator
finishes, and every deed that changes how a book stands goes through it.

The second fault was never specific to putting a book away. It had been in every fold turn since the
card's height was first carried, and only showed when one book moved while the rest stood still.

## Re-import stood the book on the shelf twice

The front matter was read, stored and paginated correctly, and still did not show. The library backup
said why: two rows for one book, the same `content_hash` on both.

    -131000000  litres  litres:art:125559      37 chapters
    -524000000  file    fb2:id:c420b97e-…      38 chapters

`BookImporting.reimport` read the kept file and installed it with a plain `BookOrigin(source: .file)`,
so `BookInstaller` worked the fingerprint out from the file. For a book that came from a service that
is a different name than the one it is already filed under, and a different name is a different work
id. The re-import landed beside the book it was meant to correct, and the reader went on reading the
old one.

It now carries the held `LocalBookRecord` into the origin: the fingerprint, the source, the source id
and the archive hash. `BookOrigin` gained `archiveHash` for the last of those, since the container a
book arrived in is long gone by the time its file is read again.

The `deduplicating` flag went with it. Only the re-import ever passed `false`, and the check it turned
off is the one that would have caught this.

## What FB2 was throwing away

`XMLParser` hands text over as characters with no tags in them, so every `<emphasis>` and `<strong>` in
a body was flattened. The parser already had the shape of a fix: a note's marker was kept by recording
where its `<a>` opened and closed and weaving the tags back in afterwards. That is now one `Mark` for
all three, held on a stack so one inside another comes out nested rather than crossed, and the words
themselves are still untouched, since a reading position counts them. On the book that started this:
155 italics and 16 bolds where there had been none.

Epigraphs were centred, which is wrong for what they are. A paragraph inside an `<epigraph>` or a
`<cite>` is a passage quoted rather than told, and is now held off the edge the way the reader already
holds one. That book has two epigraphs over its first page, a sixty-line list of the schools of magic
and an inscription on a monument: 65 inset paragraphs where the old reading had none. `<poem>` keeps
its centring, which is the one place the file's own shape asks for it.

## A number that reads the books again

None of that reaches a book already on the shelf. A chapter holds what the parser made of its file at
the time, so re-measuring sets the same words again and cannot add words it never held, and until now
the only way back was the reader pressing Re-import on one book at a time.

`BookReading.version` is that way back. `local_book.reading_version` dates each copy with the build that
read it, every install writes the current number, and a book below it is read again from its kept file
at launch, one at a time at utility priority. Raising the number is the whole of it. Changes to how text
is set rather than to what it holds still belong in `Typography.version` and `ChapterLayout.rulesVersion`,
which re-measure without reading anything again.

The two housekeeping passes were folded into one task and put in order. Dropping a book the device
holds twice goes first, so nothing is read again for a book about to be dropped.

## The cover a book grows out of

Opening a book from its own page grew the reader out of `CoverAnchor`, but the cover itself went on
being drawn behind it, so the growing screen and the cover it grew out of were both on the page at once,
and on the way back the cover reappeared before the zoom had finished.

The shelf had solved this already: `BookView.face(during:)` takes the panels down and puts an anchor in
their place. `CoverAnchor` now answers the same three points of a zoom. It carries the whole cover as
one picture rather than only its printed face, since the reading mark and the binding are drawn over
that face in SwiftUI and a zoom that shrank into a cover without them would have shown the mark leave.

## The sweep, and what it cost

Reading every book again at launch was turned on and then turned off the same evening, after it took
reading positions off five books that had been read through.

`placeInTheBook` works the reader's place out as an absolute character by summing the lengths of the
chapters before theirs, and counted an unmeasured one as nothing. For a book imported by a build that
never recorded `textLength`, every chapter before the reader's counted as nothing, so the place came out
as the offset inside its own chapter and `carry` put the reader in chapter one. The device's own store
shows it plainly: positions written during the sweep, six of them on the first chapter, two carrying
offsets of 1620 and 3436, which are in-chapter offsets applied to the front of the book.

It now refuses rather than guesses. Where any chapter before the reader's is unmeasured it reports
nothing, and `carry` leaves the position exactly where it was.

That fed the next thing. A book whose progress dips below the read threshold and comes back is dated as
just read, so it stood out as a cover again on a shelf it had left. `BookReading.version` and its column
stay, and so does `rereadWhatIsBehind()`; what is off is the call at launch. Re-import by hand does the
same work on one book.

`BookInstaller` also dated every book it read as changed today, which sorts the library's cards, so
re-reading a library shuffled it under the reader. It keeps what the book already had.

## Where a book's cover went

Half the shelf came up with bare spines. The covers were all on the disk, 519 files for 519 books. What
had moved was the path: an imported book's `coverURL` is stored absolute, into the app's own container,
and the system hands out a new container on every install. Books the sweep had re-read carried the
current one and drew; the 272 it had not still named a container from an earlier install and drew
nothing. A dozen installs in one evening is what made it obvious.

The store now works a local book's cover path out from its number as it reads the book. `SpinePress`
also went from taking only what was already cached to reading that file, since a book on its edge never
decodes its own cover and a spine printed without one is filed as printed and never asked for again.

## One rule for whether a book is in play

A book was being drawn on its edge and kept on the Reading shelf at the same time. Two rules were
asking different questions: the shelf asked whether the reader was in the book, and the filter asked
whether they had finished it. A book with nothing read against it answers no to both.

`Book.standsOnItsEdge()` is now the one rule, and the shelf, the filter and the menu all read it.
Read to the end also stopped being a question about the character count: a book on the Finished shelf
is finished whatever its progress, which is what the reader said, and progress is derived and can be
lost where a shelf cannot.

"Put the book away" marks a book read as well as filing it, and is offered on any book the Reading
shelf is holding rather than only on one already read through. Whatever that shelf keeps now carries a
way off it.

## An epigraph belongs to the chapter it stands over

A reported page was a whole chapter holding nothing but the two epigraphs its book opens with, headed
by the book's own title because the page had no name of its own. The epigraphs reach the book at all
because a body is opened the way a part is, and a part's front matter becomes a page.

That suits a part, which names its page, and not a body, which has nothing to name one with: the
contents gains an entry with no title on it, and a turn lands on four lines. Front matter carrying no
title now leads the section opening under it, so it arrives after that chapter's heading and before
its text. A plate is exempt, being something to look at rather than something to read.

`BookReading.version` goes to 3, and something now acts on it. The launch sweep stays off; the re-read
moved instead to where the reader asks for the book. `ReaderScreen.Model.loadIfNeeded()` reads a book
whose copy is behind this build again from its kept file, before it takes the chapters out of the store.

That is the same work cut down to the one book somebody is waiting for. Reading a library at once is
what made it expensive, since it wrote positions for books nobody had opened. The carry those positions
needed was fixed in the same pass that turned the launch call off, so what closed the sweep down is the
part now covered.

## Left undone

- The reader's chrome is still left-to-right for an RTL book: the progress bar fills from the left and
  the contents reads the usual way. Only the page and its turn are mirrored.
- *Dracula*'s human contents page survives the contents test, because its lines are chapter numbers
  where the navigation's entries are numbers plus titles.
- Tables flatten to paragraphs and math is dropped. Both are rare in fiction and neither is cheap: see
  the note on drawing a table at layout time rather than rendering it at import.
