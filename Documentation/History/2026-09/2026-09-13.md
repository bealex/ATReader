# 2026-09-13

## Controls a bar would not move

The reader's controls sat a bar's height below the book's name, and the lift written to raise them
never applied. The bar rebuilds a `ToolbarItem`'s content as its own button, centres it on its height,
and reads no geometry modifier that came in with it. Measured off the device, the glyphs stood at
exactly the bar's middle, 22 points below the safe area, whether the lift asked for 8.65 points or
13.65.

So the reader draws them itself. `GlassRow` is a row of glyph buttons under one pane of glass, in
`DesignSystem`, and a row of one is a circle because a capsule as tall as it is wide is one. The reader
puts Close in one and the bookmark, the contents, the appearance sheet and the debug button in another,
and stands the row on the line the running head is set on: `controlsTop` is the head's own inset plus
half the difference between the line it is set on and the height of a control. Aligning the line boxes
rather than the ink is what makes it hold at any type size, and for the head it also lands on the
middle of the capitals, the ink sitting lower only where a word carries a descender.

Measured on a rendered page, with the head's ink at 70.67 to 83.33 points: the head's middle is 77.00
and the controls' is 77.17.

Sizes came off the screenshot of what the system drew: a 44-point pane, glyphs at 20 points, 18 points
in from the edge of the screen. `Design.Size.touch` is 45 and `Design.Size.glyph(in:)` of it is 20.25,
so the lattice carries all three within a point.

The stack stays, with its bar hidden, because `readerBarAppearance` reaches the window through it.

## A status bar nobody owned

`statusBarHidden` was on the reader all along and did nothing, because nobody asked it. An
over-full-screen presentation leaves the status bar with the screen underneath, and
`modalPresentationCapturesStatusBarAppearance` on the presented screen is what moves it.

## A scene break that arrives justified

A reported page had its `* * *` at the margin. The service sends that paragraph as
`<p style="text-align:justify">`, and centring came only from the markup saying `center`.

Counted over the 12,212 chapters in the library backup: 41,004 scene breaks come marked `center`, 589
come with no alignment at all, and 25 come justified, those last in four books. The rule is now the
text rather than the markup: a paragraph of nothing but asterisks, seven at most, stands centred
wherever it is found.

## Putting a book away

A book read to its end stands out as a cover for a day. `put_away_at` records a reader who's done with
it now, and `isPutAway` holds only while it is later than the last reading and the last time out, so
opening the book again puts it back on the board for its day. The menu offers it on a book that is read
through, written through, has nothing new in it and is still out.

`isStandingOut` is the one rule both sides read: out as a cover if it was read or asked for within the
day and not put away since. The shelf stands a book on its edge when it isn't standing out, and a
folded run keeps only what is. So with the series folded away, a book put away leaves the shelf
altogether rather than turning to a spine on it, which is what a fold means.

The spine is printed before the turn starts. A book turns onto whatever picture is hanging, and the
press only starts printing when the edge panel comes into view, so the book turned onto a bare board
and its spine arrived afterwards. The menu asks `SpinePress.printed` for that one spine and puts the
book away when it comes back, by which time `SpinePrint` has it filed and the turn hangs the real one.

## A book with nothing to grow out of

A screen presented with no zoom can't be dragged shut: the drag down that closes a book is the zoom
transition's own interactive dismissal. So every way into the reader now names what it grows out of.

From the shelf's menu that is the book itself, handed over the way a tap hands it over. A book standing
on its edge grows out of its spine rather than its cover, because a cover is neither decoded nor drawn
while a book stands that way and a picture of it would be the bare board. `BookView` picks the side
from `turned`, for the anchor's width as well as its picture.

From a book's own page it is the cover at the top. `CoverAnchor` is a view standing behind that cover
carrying the printed face, which the zoom grows out of and shrinks back into, and which UIKit hides for
as long as the book is open. The reader opened from the Read button, from a chapter and from a bookmark
all take it.

## A chevron that turned nothing

An author's header offers to turn their shelf round. With the series folded away the shelf holds only
what is being read, nothing stands on its edge, and the turn changes nothing. `ShelfView.Contents.turns`
answers whether any book on the shelf is shelved, and the header shows the chevron, takes the tap and
carries the button trait only when it does.
