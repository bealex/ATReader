# 2026-09-13

Reading EPUB, and what the reader had to learn to show one: emphasis, lists, and books written from
the right. Zip64 came along with it.

## What the format gave away for free

The spine and the navigation between them state what `FB2Parser` infers. One document is one chapter,
in the order the spine lists, named by the navigation and standing at the depth it gives. `ParsedBook`
already carried a `level` that nothing had ever filled in meaningfully; this fills it.

Three-quarters of the integration was already there. `BookInstaller`, `LocalBookFiles`, the numbering,
the deduplication, re-import, the prepared-text and measurement caches are all format-neutral, and
`BookImporting.formats` was already an array. `ZipArchive` needed a way to fetch a member by name and
nothing else structural.

## Where the work actually was

`EpubContent`, and it was all of it. Three things went wrong before it read a real book, and none of
them showed up against a file written to match the specification.

**`XMLParser` could not open any of the four books.** Every one of them names entities that only the
HTML DTD ever declared, and one undeclared entity fails the whole document. Rewriting the reduction on
top of a scanner cost more than the rest of the format put together.

**`<meta>` is void in HTML and not in an EPUB package.** EPUB 3 states half of what it knows as the
text inside a `<meta>`. Treating it as void, which the HTML element list says to, silently swallowed
`rendition:layout` and every refinement, so fixed-layout books read as ordinary ones. The test caught
it; nothing else would have.

**`"#n1".split(separator: "#")` gives one piece, not two.** Empty subsequences are dropped by default,
so every footnote marker pointing inside its own document was read as an ordinary link and thrown away.

## Chaptering, three attempts

1. **Cut at headings.** Wrong, and worse, quietly wrong: the level was read off the `h` rather than the
   digit after it, so the heading list came back empty and every document stayed whole. Project
   Gutenberg puts fourteen chapters in one file, so *A Journey to the Centre of the Earth* came out as
   four chapters of 130,000 characters.
2. **Cut at the shallowest heading level.** Better, but a file opening with the book's title in an
   `<h1>` and its chapters in `<h2>` cut into two pieces: the title, and everything else.
3. **Cut where the navigation says.** Right, and it is the format's own answer. `EpubContent` marks the
   blocks the navigation's anchors name, `EpubSections` cuts there, and headings are only the fallback
   for a document the navigation names once or not at all.

Two rules had to be added on top, both from looking at real files:

- Gutenberg lists a chapter's number and its title as two navigation entries, so cutting there gives
  alternating stubs and bodies. A stub is folded into the chapter it heads and both names are kept.
  The first version of this rule folded any piece under 60 characters, which swallowed short real
  chapters; a stub is now a piece under 60 characters **with no paragraph in it**, which is what a
  heading standing alone actually looks like.
- A contents page is a page whose lines are mostly titles the navigation also lists. Testing the
  structure rather than the page's name means it holds in any language.

## Measurements

Four Project Gutenberg books, read on the Mac in the package tests:

| book | chapters | characters | pictures |
|---|---|---|---|
| *A Journey to the Centre of the Earth* | 51 | 498,433 | 4 |
| *Dracula* | 36 | 851,999 | 2 |
| *The Odyssey* | 32 | 695,573 | 1 |
| *Time Crime* | 8 | 230,515 | 15 |

All four read in about 0.9 seconds together, which is the whole suite rather than one book.

*Time Crime* comes out as 8 chapters because its navigation has five entries: it is a magazine serial,
and the file genuinely says nothing about where its scenes divide. That is the source's limit, not the
reader's.

## What the reader learned

- **Emphasis.** `BookHTML` already fenced `<sub>` and `<sup>` with private-use characters so their
  places survived the flattening. The same machinery now carries `<em>` and `<strong>` as `StyleMark`s,
  with a depth count per kind so a stretch nested inside another of its own kind closes once.
  `ChapterPagination` adds the trait to whatever face already stands there, so a phrase set both ways
  comes out as both.
- **Lists.** The item's mark goes into its own text rather than beside it, because a reading position
  counts the characters of a chapter and anything the page shows has to be among them. Only the indent
  is left to say, and `ParagraphRuler` gained `headIndent` for it: until now only a first line was ever
  indented.
- **Right to left.** The direction rides in the markup as `dir="rtl"` per block, so a chapter body
  still holds everything needed to set it. `ParagraphRuler` has to put the direction back on the text
  it measures, because the paragraph style is stripped before CoreText sees it and without it a line
  opening on a Latin word inside an Arabic paragraph is set the wrong way round. `PageTurnView` mirrors
  every horizontal distance it reads or draws, which turned out to be four places.

`Typography.version` and `ChapterLayout.rulesVersion` are both bumped: the prepared text carries marks
it did not before, and where a line stands has changed.

## Two UI tests that were already failing

`BookmarkUITests` and `ReaderControlsUITests.testMiddleTapTogglesTheControls` were failing before any
of this. Stashing the whole day's work and running them against the unmodified tree gave the same four
assertions at the same lines, which is what settled it.

Both came from one thing, and it was a real defect rather than a test artefact.

**Faded out is not gone.** The reader's controls were hidden with `.opacity(0)` and
`.allowsHitTesting(false)`. A view at no opacity is still in the accessibility tree: VoiceOver would
offer the reader buttons that are not on screen, and a test looking for `Appearance` found it. The
diagnostic that settled it printed `exists=true hittable=false`, which says exactly that the view was
correctly inert and still findable.

`.accessibilityHidden(isChromeHidden)` did not fix it. `GlassRow` puts its buttons under
`.glassEffect`, and the flag does not reach through. The controls are now built only while they are up,
with the fade kept as a transition.

**A button that does nothing.** With the controls reachable again, `BookmarkUITests` still failed: a
book opens on its title page, that page carries none of a chapter's text, so `displayedRanges` is empty
and `toggleBookmark()` makes no mark. The reader was offering an `Add bookmark` button that did
nothing. It is now disabled where no mark can stand, and the test steps past the title page the way
`CatalogUITestCase.turnPage` already says reading tests must.

A run of the two suites took 745 seconds while failing and 137 seconds once fixed. Nearly all of that
was XCUITest waiting out timeouts on queries that could never match.

## Warnings and dead code

Six compiler warnings went: two unused `UIView?` results from the zoom source, two `guard let layout`
bindings nothing read, a `weak` capture that differed from the strong one around it, and a `bind`
overload that had lost its `@discardableResult`.

One dead declaration went with them: `ProfileScreen.Component` read `BookInbox` out of the environment
and never used it. `BookholdApp.appDelegate` looks equally unused and is not: it is how SwiftUI
installs an app delegate at all.

## Reading a book the way its own publisher set it

Four things, all of them visible on the first EPUB anyone opened.

**Paragraphs now follow the tradition of the language.** Russian indents the first line and leaves no
gap; English parts its paragraphs with space and indents nothing. Before this every book got the
indent, whatever it was written in.

**Whose heading the page shows is settled by what the body wrote it as.** The Odyssey came out with
`CHAPTER 6 / THE ODYSSEY. BOOK I` above a body that opened by repeating both lines, because Project
Gutenberg names a chapter's number and its title as two navigation anchors and the stub-folding put
them together. The existing dropper only matched the number and the title read as one string, so it
caught nothing here. Now the opening blocks are matched against the title alone as well, and where the
book wrote them as headings the reader's own heading goes instead of theirs. Titles standing in the
body had no face of their own until now, which is why they read as centred body text.

**A book's own contents page is dropped.** Dracula's survived the first rule, since its lines read
`CHAPTER I. Jonathan Harker's Journal` where the navigation says `CHAPTER I JONATHAN HARKER'S
JOURNAL`. The rule that catches it is structural: most of a contents page's words stand inside links
into the book, and no chapter reads like that.

**Links work.** A `<a href>` into the book becomes a `LinkMark` on the paragraph and a `data-anchor` on
the block it lands on, so a book's own index is navigable. Where a link lands is only known after the
whole book has been read, so the addresses are scanned out of the markup in a pass before anything is
reduced.

That turned up a bug in the note handling: every short block standing under an id was remembered as a
possible note, so a link's target was being carried into the chapter that linked to it and shown as a
note. Notes are now limited to the blocks something pointed at as a note.

Counts across the four books on hand, after all of it:

| book | chapters | links | anchors |
|---|---|---|---|
| *A Journey to the Centre of the Earth* | 51 | 10 | 53 |
| *Dracula* | 35 | 0 | 27 |
| *The Odyssey* | 32 | 374 | 368 |
| *Time Crime* | 8 | 0 | 0 |

Dracula's links fall to nothing because the page holding them was its contents.

## Left undone

- The reader's chrome is still left-to-right for an RTL book: the progress bar fills from the left and
  the contents reads the usual way. Only the page and its turn are mirrored.
- *Dracula*'s human contents page survives the contents test, because its lines are chapter numbers
  where the navigation's entries are numbers plus titles.
- Tables flatten to paragraphs and math is dropped. Both are rare in fiction and neither is cheap: see
  the note on drawing a table at layout time rather than rendering it at import.
