# 2026-09-30

## A page turn that jumped

A turn often failed to slide. Two causes, both in `PageTurner` as it came over from SwiftUI.

The turner took a sheet out of the window once it was off screen, and UIKit only updates and draws a
view that is in one. The controller hands the next step whichever of its three sheet views is free,
so the page sliding in was the one that view showed two turns ago, until the next update cycle set it
right halfway through the turn. The two sheets not in view now stay in the window at no opacity.

Any change to the model reached the turner as a reload, and a reload set every sheet's transform
again, outside the animation, which ends it where it's going. The model changes during most turns: it
cuts the next sheets in the background, moves the book's length, and hides the controls when a turn
begins. A reload that arrives while a turn is settling now waits for it to land, and a layout pass
only rearranges the sheets when the turner's size has changed.

## Filming the turn

The simulator's screen, recorded with `simctl io recordVideo` while a UI test turned pages, split into
frames with `ffmpeg` and laid out in numbered sheets, showed what the change above had missed. Taps
turned cleanly. A forward swipe showed the next page covering the screen on its first frame, then
sliding back towards the finger and in again: the sheet had waited at rest, which is the place a page
stands when it's in front, and a drag, unlike a tap, never set it at the turn's start. A sheet taking a
part in a turn is now set there first.

Turning onto a sheet still being cut landed on the old page: the model waits for the new one with its
current sheet unchanged, and the turner put that sheet back in front. The blank sheet the turn brought
in now stays until the text fills it.

A drag used to animate the page from the edge to the finger over 0.3 s, re-aimed at every event, which
reads as the page being thrown under the finger. A chase on an update link, closing the gap with a time
constant of 70 ms, went next and moved the page while the finger rested. Now only the finger moves it,
by as far as it moved times one plus ten times the share of the width still between them, and it
follows the finger exactly once it's within half a point.

The page number on an incoming page still changes about 80 ms after it lands, when the model counts the
turn and moves the book's length.

## A book as thick as what's been read

The opening's cover used to swing open as a board with no depth, its back a flat grey. It now carries
the pages already read with it, and its back shows the page before the one opened. Most of what went
wrong on the way was the shelf showing through.

- The reader paints its paper on its own view and leaves the sheets clear, so the picture of the page
  before was see-through everywhere but the text.
- Setting each back strip in by the leaf's depth along its own angle pulled neighbouring strips apart
  on a curled leaf, leaving thin gaps down the page. The back face is now offset at the joints.
- Turning the leaf about its cover face left its page face past the spine as it came over, lying across
  the edge of the page it uncovered. It turns about the page face now.
- The ends of the pages, drawn with a pattern colour, let the shelf through between the lines once the
  layer was turned in 3D. A picture over an opaque fill doesn't.
- Every edge came out black in light mode. The paper colour, a SwiftUI colour bridged to UIKit, came
  back black from `withAlphaComponent(1).cgColor`; it's rebuilt from its components now. It was also
  taken once when the book opened, so a theme switched while reading shut the book in the old paper.

A drag down that comes back up past where it began shows the whole spread: the book is still a little
short of full size while the leaf already lies flat. Two changes to the drag, one limiting how far the
finger moves the book and one damping the carry on release, didn't touch that and were taken back.
The face at the spine end, which showed as a dark wedge between the two pages, is now drawn only while
the cover faces the eye.

Profiled on the simulator by `OpeningFrameCostTests`: laying the book out took 1.8 ms and a frame
0.19 ms at the median and 0.41 ms at worst, against 8.3 ms a frame at 120 Hz. The frame that took in
the page before cost 16.2 ms, on top of the reader's own drawing of it, because the picture was drawn
once and then drawn again mirrored. Drawn once into a mirrored context, that frame is 3.5 ms including
the page.

`DevicePageTests.everyReportReproducesItsMeasure` fails on `c7479bd`, before this change, with a page
laid out 11 points shorter than its report. The package unit tests don't build on it either:
`ProgressBar.swift` imports UIKit, which the macOS package build can't resolve.

## Profiling the opening for 120 Hz

Profiled on the largest simulator with an optimised build, a probe timing every frame of the run and
Time Profiler attached, with the samples XCTest put on the main thread left out.

- Every opening dropped frames 4% of the way in, where the chapter was typeset on the main thread.
  `ChapterLayout.prepare` is main-actor isolated and called the nonisolated `ChapterPagination.typeset`
  directly; it now sets the text in a `@concurrent` function. The chapter is still typeset a second
  time, on the composer's actor, which needs its own copy.
- The back of the leaf cost 8 to 16 ms in the frame it arrived, and the same before a shut's first
  frame. Rendering the reader's page and then mirroring it was 16 ms by itself. The back strips now show
  the page layer's own contents, laid the other way round so they read unmirrored. That frame is under
  2 ms, and laying a shut out went from 9 to 17 ms down to 2 to 5.
- A monochrome picture was resampled from its full size on every draw of its page, about 8 ms for a
  plate. Its shading is now resampled once, off the main actor, when the page is cut. The first try kept
  the resampled copy and still sampled: the picture landed at a fractional pixel. Snapping its rect to
  whole pixels and turning interpolation off made the draw a copy.
- Each page gathered its whole text for VoiceOver on every change and redrew on every layout pass.
  It gathers the text when asked, and redraws when its size changes.
- The reader set out three sheets in one frame as it arrived. It now sets out the one in front first
  and the others a frame or two later.

Late frames the probe kept reporting at a quarter of the way in, and ten of them through the first
opening, were XCTest's accessibility snapshots, taken as the test waited for the page.

At the end, 1 of 1,094 windows of 8.3 ms during the motion has the app busy for 7 ms or more, against
6 of 1,304 before the sheets were staggered. What remains is the first opening's caption, the frame a
shut lands and the reader is taken down, and drawing the shelf behind a cover whose artwork isn't
printed, which the cover needs before its first frame. None of it has been timed on a phone.
