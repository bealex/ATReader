# 2026-09-15

A catalogue's hits now say which of them the reader already has.

## What a catalogue gives, and what it doesn't

An OPDS entry carries a title, an author line, a summary and the links to take the book away. Nothing
in it identifies the book: the id is the catalogue's own, and two catalogues offering one book agree on
nothing but its name. So `HeldBooks` matches on the name, which makes every match a guess.

`HeldBooks` indexes the library by title, with each held book's author words filed under it. The title
has to match outright, after case, accents and punctuation are folded away. The author only has to
agree, meaning the two names share a word, because a catalogue writes a name in whatever order it
likes, half of them give no author at all, and a mark that only appeared on an exact match would almost
never appear.

Two rules came out of that:

- The index drops words shorter than three letters, so an initial decides nothing. It agrees with every
  name there is, which makes it worthless as evidence and dangerous as a veto.
- A side with no author words matches anything of that title. A catalogue that named nobody has said
  nothing to contradict, and refusing the match there would lose most of the marks worth showing.

Diacritic folding is what makes Russian names comparable across catalogues: `ё` folds to `е`, which is
the difference between two spellings of the same surname.

## Where it is read, and what actually decides

`FeedModel` refreshes the index on every feed load and again after a book lands, so a book taken from a
catalogue is marked on the row it came from without leaving the screen. `OPDSFeedScreen.Rows` draws the
pill, and both the browse screen and the search screen use those rows, so a book is marked wherever it
turns up.

Pressing a marked row still fetches the file. `BookImporting` then recognises the text by its content
hash and files it on the row the book already stands on, which is the check that decided this before
the pill existed.

## One book held twice, and a series counted two ways

A long series in a real library came out three books longer than the series is. Reading the backup's
`work` and `local_book` tables apart told what each extra was:

- Two copies of one book downloaded from the same shop under different ids, whose files differ, so
  neither the source id nor the content hash pairs them.
- A book held once as a file and once as a service work.
- A hand-added FB2 sitting beside the service's copy of the same volume.

The fourth surprise wasn't a duplicate. The service carries a book of that series the other shop
doesn't list at all, which accounts for a place nothing else explains.

### Which copy the shelf keeps

The rule is now whether the book is written to its end rather than how far it's been read: a finished
book is better held as a file, which opens with no network and can't be withdrawn, and a book still
being written stays with the service, whose copy is the only one that grows.

`BookInstaller` files every import as `isFinished: true`, so a file's own flag says nothing about the
book and only the service copy's word is read. That's what `preferred(_:over:)` asks.

### The two scales

The service gives every volume its own place, so a two-volume work takes two; a file gives the work
one place however far it runs. That library's files counted to seven and its service works counted
from eleven, which is what left places eight to ten empty and stood two different books on seven,
and two more on twelve.

`SeriesScale` walks the file-numbered works in order to learn where each one starts on the service's
scale, then walks everything by that start, taking off the volumes that ran ahead. What it settles on,
and what it costs a library holding little of a series, is in `Architecture.md`.

Three things only showed up once it was built:

- The scale has to be published both ways. Folding a work's volumes onto one place is right for
  reading, but pairing tells two books apart by their volume, and fed the folded number it merged the
  two halves of a two-volume work into one book. `volumes(of:)` is the printed place and
  `volumesApart(of:)` counts each volume separately.
- The file sitting beside the service's copy was never a numbering fault. The title key stripped a
  bracketed aside, so a volume written in brackets and the same volume written after a full stop
  spelled different keys, and the two never met to be compared. An aside naming a volume is kept now.
- A title that is nothing but a volume word and a figure strips to an empty key, which filed every
  such book as one work and swallowed the gaps between them. `LibraryFilterTests` caught it with
  "Книга 1" and "Книга 5". Such a title keeps the whole of itself, and its figure counts the series
  rather than the work.

A file's own figure is worth less than it looks. One of those files numbered its book seventh where
the series puts it eighth, colliding with the book that really is seventh.
