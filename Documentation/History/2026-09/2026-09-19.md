# 2026-09-19

A lighter flick turns the page, and lowering the threshold showed two things the old one had been
hiding.

## The threshold

A flick landed a turn only above 1200pt/s, counted as 120pt of travel projected over a tenth of a
second. That's a full swipe. It's 30 now, 300pt/s, against the 20 that cancels a turn flicked back.

## A short flick turned two pages

Flicking back and then forward in quick succession often went back one page and forward two. A trace
of the turn's state on a device showed the second turn coming from the page's tap:

- A flick that travelled 5, 16 or 26pt ended as a drag that landed, and the same touch fired the tap a
  millisecond later. The tap found a turn running and queued another.
- Flicks of 39pt and over never fired it, and no flick back did at any length.

The pan has watched touches without taking them since it replaced `DragGesture` on
[the 12th](2026-09-12.md), so nothing has made the tap fail since then. Under the old threshold the
same flicks fell back as drags and turned the page as taps, which read as working. The second flick
of a quick pair comes out short, which is why it took two close together to see it.

`PageTurnView` now drops the tap from a touch it has taken as a drag. The flag is cleared when the
next finger comes down, which `HorizontalPan` reports. Clearing it when the drag ends would race the
tap, and clearing it in the tap would leave it set after every drag too long to fire one.

## A finger arriving on a turn still running

Found by reading before the trace came back, and seen once in it. A turn let go of runs on for 0.22s
with `turn` still set, and a drag beginning inside that window steered the old turn. A flick forward
pulled the page going back in again, the old animation's completion then moved the index under it,
and a flick that ended inside the window was judged as that turn's flick back and dropped.

The turn let go of is now a `Settle`, and a finger arriving while one is set lands it at once and
takes a page of its own from its next move. Each carries an id, because the completion of a turn
landed early still fires, 50ms later in the trace, by which time the next one is settling.

## What the simulator couldn't say

XCUITest waits for the app to idle before every gesture, which put taps 1.1s apart, so two flicks
inside a quarter of a second can't be written as a UI test. The bundled book also fails to open under
`-at-ui-test-guest` on the iPhone 17 simulator: the cover raises an "Error" alert and the page never
draws. `ReaderSwipeUITests` opens the book the same way. Not looked into.

## The reader on an iPhone Duo

Xcode 27.1 ships guidance for the folding iPhone as an assistant skill, `app-resizability`, inside
`IDEIntelligenceChat.framework`. What it says that mattered here: the Duo stands a vertical bar down
one side in place of the bars at the top, the bar arrives as a side inset for the life of the scene,
and which side is asked through `toolbarVerticalEdge` or `traitCollection.verticalBarEdge` rather than
worked out from insets, size or orientation.

Measured on the simulator, unfolded and on its side: a 951 by 669 window with insets of 0 at the top,
34 at the foot and 84 trailing. The clock and the network ring take the top 98pt of that column, the
system's own toolbar pill starts at 120pt and stands 24pt in from the edge, and its tab pill ends 25pt
above the foot. Hiding the status bar takes the column away and the trailing inset with it.

Three things were wrong:

- **Every page stood 17pt high.** The head was 28pt from the top and the page number 76pt from the
  foot. The cause is in `Reader.md`; the measurement that found it was the page's frame in the window,
  `(0, -17, 867, 669)` where it should have been the window's own. An iPhone 17 gives `(0, 14, …)` for
  the same reason and then hides it, because `ignoresSafeArea` widens the frame back up to the top.
- **The controls crossed the top of the page.** `DeviceBand` inferred the side from the insets and
  only in a window taller than it is wide, so the unfolded screen read as an ordinary one.
- **The status bar stayed up while reading**, since it hid only under a notch.

The status bar hides with the controls now, and the first version of that held the bar's column open
at the width the window last reported, the way a notch's band is always there. It read as a fault: an
empty 84pt strip down one side of a page with nothing in it. The page is set to the whole width
instead, 319 pages of the bundled book where the held column made 334, and the bar overlays it.

`UIViewController.preferredVerticalBarBehavior` and SwiftUI's `toolbarVerticalBehavior(.disabled)` turn
the vertical bar off for a screen, and the header names a full-screen player as the case for it. Not
taken: it puts the status bar back across the top, where hiding it moves the page as it does on an
iPad, and the controls were wanted down the side.

`simctl io screenshot` returns a black frame for the Duo's default display. The inner screen is one of
the framebuffer ports `simctl io enumerate` lists, and `--display=<port UUID>` captures it.
`-at-ui-test-reader -1000000` opens the bundled book straight into the reader, which is how every
picture here was taken without a tap.

## What one page costs to set

Asked because a page that changes width sets the whole book again, and the way out is to set only the
pages being read. Timed with generated text at the test context, a 402 by 874 page at 19pt, seven runs
each, on the Mac under the simulator. Milliseconds, median:

| | pages | preparing | laying out | a page, both | a page, laying out |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Debug, one paragraph | 1 | 2.5 | 4.8 | 7.3 | 4.8 |
| Debug, 40 paragraphs | 27 | 79 | 77 | 5.8 | 2.9 |
| Debug, 160 paragraphs | 107 | 321 | 273 | 5.6 | 2.5 |
| optimised, one paragraph | 1 | 1.8 | 2.9 | 4.7 | 2.9 |
| optimised, 40 paragraphs | 27 | 61 | 37 | 3.6 | 1.4 |
| optimised, 160 paragraphs | 107 | 242 | 122 | 3.4 | 1.1 |

Preparing is parsing and hyphenating, which knows nothing of the page and is kept with the chapter, so
a change of width pays only for laying out. That is 3ms for a page set on its own, where the four
workers have nothing to share, and about 1ms a page across a chapter where they do.

The text was short nonsense words with no notes or pictures, so a real page will cost somewhat more.
The harness was a throwaway suite calling `ChapterContent.prepare` and `ChapterLayout.make`.

## A book cut into pages around the reader

A folding screen changes the page's size whenever it opens or shuts, and every change of size laid the
whole book out again before the page could settle: `BookPagination` measured every chapter in order,
because a chapter's first page depended on where the one before it ended. So the book is no longer
measured at all.

`PageCutter` cuts one page at a time, forwards from where it starts or backwards from where it ends,
weighing it against the two pages beyond it instead of against the whole chapter. The cost of a page,
its rules and its squared shortfall, is the same function both ways; only the search runs the other way
round, and a page past the lines composed is taken to be finished by lines nobody has composed yet.
Backwards, a chapter's start is the one hard edge, and a short opening sinks to the foot of its page.

`ChapterLayout` composes only the paragraphs around the page asked for, on a `ColumnComposer.Setter`
that keeps its own copy of the chapter off the main actor. Lines are numbered from the first paragraph
composed, so a number keeps naming the same line as composing reaches forwards and backwards. The
title's air that goes at the head of a page is taken off per page now rather than out of the line,
since the same chapter can open a page read forwards and run on under another read backwards.

`BookLayout` strings pages across chapters, runs a section on under the end of the chapter before it in
either direction, and stands a page of its own where a chapter can't be fetched. The reader keeps a run
of sheets it has shown, so turning back and forth shows the same pages; `PageTurnView` is handed one
sheet and its neighbours, and every turn lands past the end of it.

Measured in Debug on the simulator, opening a 160-paragraph chapter in its middle: 48 ms to the first
page, then about 6 ms a turn either way, 17 ms for the first turn back, which composes three pages
behind the anchor. A UI probe turned fourteen pages forward and fourteen back through the bundled book
and saw the same pages in reverse, down to the title page.

`chapter_column` and `chapter_placement` are dropped by a migration, with `ColumnStore`,
`PlacementStore` and the layout fingerprint that filed them. `chapter_content` stays: preparing the text
is the larger share of opening a chapter, and it depends on nothing about the page.

### The page number went with it

A page number needs every page before it cut. The foot of a page now carries a bar, the share of the
book up to the end of the page, counted in characters, and how many pages the book comes to at this
setting: characters of ordinary prose a line holds, measured on a sample in the book's language, times
the lines a page holds, times 0.99.

That estimate runs well short of the pages a book really cuts into. The bundled book said "page 1 of
254" on the iPhone 17 simulator and now says "(154)"; unfolded on the Duo it says 177. It is a book of
short paragraphs of dialogue, verse and pictures, and every paragraph leaves most of its last line
empty, which a count of characters can't see.

### Tests

`BookLayoutTests` walks a generated book forwards from its title page and backwards from its last, and
holds both walks to covering every character of every chapter once, fitting the page, and leaving no
widow or orphan at a break inside a chapter. `RunOnTests` checks the shared page from both sides.
`ColumnCacheTests` and the fingerprint tests went with what they tested; the spellings the settings are
written under are kept in `SettingSpellingTests`.

`DevicePageTests.everyReportReproducesItsMeasure` fails on all five reports under `Fixtures/Reports`,
before this change as after it. They were taken between the 3rd and the 13th, and the running-head band
grew on the 14th, so every one of them comes out 11pt shorter than the device measured.

## Deploying

`Scripts/app.sh deploy` with no target goes to the device `.env` names as `AT_DEVICE_ID`. Naming a
simulator or a device now chooses that target as well, so `deploy --device-id` can't land on a
simulator.
