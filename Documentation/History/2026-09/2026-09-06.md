# 2026-09-06

## A design system that was only an inventory

The first pass at a design system recorded what the app already did: every semantic colour, every text
style, every one of thirty-one spacing values, with counts and call sites. It was accurate and it was
useless. An inventory of unstructured values is still unstructured. Naming `spacing: 7` doesn't make 7
belong anywhere.

It was also drawn as a web page, which is the wrong specimen for an iOS app twice over. The cover
component rendered wrong in HTML (a positioning wrapper picked up the mark's own background and painted
a second disc behind it), and the cover it drew was the placeholder state, since a real cover is a
fitted image whose height comes from the artwork. Neither mistake is possible in a specimen the app
draws itself.

## What replaced it

Two rules, and everything follows from them.

Every length is a multiple of three. The old set held 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7, 8, 9, 10, 12, 14, 16, 18, 20,
22, 24, 28, 32 and 40; the new one holds 3, 6, 9, 12, 18, 24 and 36. Sizes moved with it: the chapter
mark from 16 to 18, the file mark from 19 to 18, bar buttons from 34 to 36, a row cover from 64 to 66,
the ring stroke from 3.2 to 3, the chapter mark stroke from 2 to 3. The hairline stays at 0.5, because
it's a device pixel and not a measurement.

Five colours carry a fact, down from eight. Indigo became `caution` for anything standing between the
reader and the page, which covers both the costs-money badge and a paid chapter. Pink and orange
retired: likes and Ongoing are facts, not states, so they take the neutral tint and let the glyph do
the work. One opacity, 0.15, now serves every tinted ground and every edge, replacing 0.1, 0.14 and
0.15.

Nine text roles replaced fifteen style-and-weight combinations. Footnote folded into caption, and the
weight variants on subheadline and caption2 collapsed to one each.

## What stayed out

The reader page. Its type, margins, letter spacing and five tints are settings, and a system imposed on
them would be a system imposed on someone else's book. The same goes for the proportions inside a
cover, which are ratios rather than lengths, and for motion, whose seven timings are tuned to the
gestures that use them.

## The catalogue

`DesignSystemScreen` renders the whole system from the tokens, in the app, in Debug builds. Reached from
Profile, or from launch with `-at-design-system YES` so it needs no account. Verified on a simulator:
the lattice, the palette, the type roles and the components all draw correctly, including the cover
that the web version got wrong.

## Not done yet

Library, Search, Profile and Login still carry raw spacing values. Their colours are already on the
palette; only the measurements remain.


## Seven modules, and the three things that nearly went wrong

The app was two modules: a service package and everything else. It is now seven, with `BookKit` in the
middle holding what a book is and every protocol the others meet at.

Splitting it out found things a reading would not have. `ChapterHTML` sat in the service package while
parsing HTML and knowing nothing about a service, and three files were importing the whole client for
one paragraph type. `ChapterInfo.displayTitle` was declared and never called, with its string
duplicated in two catalogues. `ZipArchive` offered four methods where two were used.

Three near-misses are worth writing down.

The old importer unpacked an archive and kept the book rather than the file, which one comment
recorded. A `read` returning only a `ParsedBook` would have kept the archive instead, and re-import
would have quietly started reading the wrong bytes. `BookFormat` returns a `ReadBook` carrying the
book and the bytes worth keeping, so the rule is in the signature.

`BookFace` and `BookWeight` feed the key every chapter's measurements are filed under. Moving them
between modules could not change their raw values, and nothing would have said so: the symptom is a
slow first page. The fingerprint was pinned in a test before the move, not after.

Renaming the store's surface to book terms caught `AppRoute.work(id:title:)` in the same pass, because
it takes an id too. The compiler found it. A rename that had matched a route with no arguments would
not have been found at all, which is the argument for doing these one narrow pattern at a time.

## What did not move, and why

`Code/Services/` still holds the session, the sweep, the badge, the background refresh and the
inbox. Each needs something only the app target has: constants a build phase writes, an identifier the
Info.plist has to agree with, the app icon, a document type. An app that owns no wiring is one whose
wiring has been hidden somewhere it cannot see what it must agree with.

The reader's own settings stayed too. What a font is went down to the typesetter; which one the reader
picked, and what it is called on screen, is the app's.
